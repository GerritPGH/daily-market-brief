Section 1What matters today

Friday gave markets a weak jobs number, and shares liked it more than bonds did. US payrolls rose by only 29,000 in September, below every forecast in Bloomberg’s survey, and unemployment edged up to 4.2% (Bloomberg, 2/10). The S&P 500 gained 0.73% to 7,722.72. The ten-year Treasury still sold off into the close and finished at 5.29%, six basis points higher on the week; John Authers says it is ‘trading like a meme stock’ (opinion). It was at 5.256% this morning (FT, 05:30). Europe is where the strain is. France’s ten-year yield briefly passed 5%, the highest since July 2002 (Trading Economics), and its gap over Germany ended the week at 140bp after the largest weekly jump in 17 years (Reuters, 5/10). The euro fell to $1.1161 this morning, its weakest since May 2025, on reports that Spain’s government is preparing for an early election (Bloomberg). The G7 agreed to release up to 100m barrels of emergency oil and diesel; Brent dipped below $100 on Friday and is at $101.68. In Brazil, Flávio Bolsonaro took about 47% of the first-round vote to President Lula’s 45%, and the two meet again on 25 Oct. Tokyo is up 2.1% and near 70,000. South Africa’s petrol price is set to pass R30 a litre on Wednesday. Today brings US ISM services at 15:00 BST (consensus 55.1) and an election in Quebec.
