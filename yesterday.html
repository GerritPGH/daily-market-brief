Section 1What matters today

The bond rout got worse. After Trump rejected Iran’s plan to reopen Hormuz, the US ten-year rose 9bp to 5.25%, a 19-year high, and the thirty-year hit 5.57%, the highest since 2004 (Bloomberg, 28/9). The thirty-year inflation-protected yield reached 3.28%, the highest since continuous issuance began in 2004 (Authers, Bloomberg 29/9). Iranian officials privately doubt a deal before the US midterms. Saudi Arabia has restored about 3.5m barrels a day through its East–West pipeline, half its capacity, so Brent eased to about $106 (Bloomberg, 28/9; TE, 29/9). The S&P 500 fell 0.8% and erased its September gain. Rate-hike bets pushed gold down again, to $4,126. In Johannesburg the All Share fell 1.55%, resources fell 5.7%, and Gold Fields fell 12% after confirming its rejected approach for Northern Star (Sharenet, 28/9). The rand is at 16.44, its weakest since early August. This morning the RBA raised rates for the fourth time this year, to 4.60%, and Tokyo fell 1.4%. From here the week turns on data: US PCE on Wednesday, the Tankan on Thursday and payrolls on Friday. China shuts for Golden Week from Thursday.
