Section 1What matters today

The bond market has taken the pen off the equity market. The US ten-year cleared 4.75% on Monday for the first time since January 2025 and is 4.778% this morning; the thirty-year is above last week’s highs with federal debt now through $40trn; the gilt reopens from the bank holiday 6bp wider at 5.208%; and the ten-year JGB is at 2.994%, its highest in more than three decades, with the market pricing a Bank of Japan hike next month at better than 90% and the yen through 160. Four major curves are pricing tightening at the same time, which has not happened once in this cycle. The accelerant is oil: Iran struck the UAE and Jordan after a US attack on an island in the Strait of Hormuz, Trump has promised a response, and Brent is $91.27. South Africa got the exact mirror of Friday’s record: the All Share fell 1.62% to 116,257, Resources −3.82%, Metals & Mining −4.53%, and yesterday’s tripwire — whether palladium held $1,400 — broke inside a session, palladium −4.57% to $1,380.75 and platinum −3.25% to $1,794.05. Tomorrow SA diesel goes up R3.15 a litre. And in the quietest item on the page, Morgan Stanley, Citi and Goldman have started telling law firms to hand back the money AI is saving them — the first shot at the leverage model that prices every professional-services firm, including the ones that audit funds. Watch the long end, not the equity screen.
