Section 1What matters today

Korea answered its own panic. On Tuesday the KOSPI fell 3.73% to 5,552.74 on AI-funding fears and a failed AI listing (Bloomberg; Trading Economics). This morning customs data showed exports for 1–20 October more than doubled to $61.6bn, with chips up 222.5% to a record $27.5bn and daily-average exports up 65.9% (Korea Customs via Korea JoongAng Daily; NewsBytes). By 09:50 Seoul time the KOSPI was up 3.28% and foreigners were net buyers (Invezz). The funding fear has not gone; it has moved into credit. CoreWeave’s bonds trade at distressed levels and tech credit-risk gauges hit fresh highs (Bloomberg). In New York the S&P 500 rose 0.28% to 7,712.57 while the Nasdaq 100 slipped 0.13% (Bloomberg). After the close SpaceX beat on profit and said it and Tesla are studying a merger (FT). Brent is back at $110.40 this morning (FT, 05:30). Two fiscal tests land today: UK September CPI and public finances at 07:00 (consensus 3.4% against 3.1% in August; FXStreet, Trading Economics), and South Africa’s medium-term budget at 14:00 SAST, with economists split between a 3.5% and a 4% deficit (IOL).
