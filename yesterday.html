Section 1What matters today

The AI boom has sent its funding bill to the market. Meta said it will need to raise “hundreds of billions of dollars” over several years, warned of an “increasingly challenging environment for raising capital” and posted its first negative quarterly free cash flow since 2012 (FT; Reuters via Benzinga). Its shares fell about 7%, its five-year CDS hit a record 114.79bp, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 2.90% to 28,959. The S&P 500 fell 1.21% to 7,663, its worst day since July (AP; Bloomberg). Money went into Treasuries: the ten-year yield fell 17bp to 4.96%, its best day since mid-2024 (cappnotes; FT). Oil rose as stocks fell. Houthi claims against Saudi targets and the evacuation of Aramco’s Dhahran headquarters lifted December Brent 4.0% to $113.17 (cappnotes; Bloomberg). Precious metals were sold for cash: silver fell 5.8%, platinum 3.6% and gold 2.8% (Trading Economics). That took the JSE Resource 10 down 3.18% and the rand to 16.51 (Sharenet). France’s ten-year premium over Bunds widened 19bp to 178bp, the most since 2011, and Moody’s rules on France tonight (Bloomberg). Overnight, Japan’s September CPI came in at 2.1% against Trading Economics’ 2.3% forecast, and the JGB ten-year fell 12bp to 2.76% (TE; FT 05:30). Today’s chains: Asian central banks spending reserves to defend their currencies, Meta’s borrowing meeting the bond market, and a software price floor that held.

Section 2World at a glance

One line per market, and the tone the tape is setting this morning.

Asia Tech rout; China in a bear market

Korea, Hong Kong and Japan fell 2.6–3.5% on Thursday. Chinese stocks are now in a bear market, with $2.4tn lost since the May peak (Bloomberg). The Philippines surprised with a 50bp hike to 5.50% as the peso hit a record low. Japan's CPI came in at 2.1%, below forecast. This morning is mixed: Hang Seng +0.58%, Nikkei −0.88% (FT 05:30). Taiwan's market is shut for Typhoon Champi (Bloomberg).

KOSPI 5,534 −3.52% · Nikkei 62,908 −2.65% · Hang Seng 22,935 −3.03%

United Kingdom Defensive FTSE; Budget in five days

The FTSE's low tech weighting limited its fall to 0.26%. Barclays will cut 5,000 jobs and booked restructuring costs of about £600m; FICC revenue fell 19% (FT; Bloomberg). Banks expect the Budget on 28 October to raise the bank surcharge from 3% to 8% (FT). Gilts rallied 4bp. Retail sales are out at 07:00.

FTSE 100 10,725 −0.26% · 10y 5.34% −4bp · GBP/USD 1.3395 −0.03%

South Africa Metals rout drags the rand

The ALSI fell 1.03% as the Resource 10 lost 3.18%: Implats fell 5.26%, Anglo American 5.24% and Sibanye 4.43%. Sasol rose 6.90% to a new high on oil (Sharenet). Clicks fell 3.56% on results (Sharenet). The Auditor-General fired its external auditor, Crowe (Business Day).

ALSI 108,525 −1.03% · USD/ZAR 16.51 +0.86% · SA 10y 8.77% +6bp

United States AI funding scare; bonds rally

Meta fell about 7% after its funding warning, and Lam Research fell more than 9% on weak guidance (Bloomberg). The Dow was flat, so the selling was concentrated in tech. Goldman's prime-brokerage clients are shorting at about five times the rate they buy, and short exposure is near a five-year high (Bloomberg). Bets on another Fed hike this year fell.

S&P 500 7,663 −1.21% · 10y 4.96% −17bp · VIX 19.03 +14.1%
