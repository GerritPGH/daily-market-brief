Section 1What matters today

The Federal Reserve raised interest rates for the first time since July 2023 — a quarter point, to 3.75–4.00%, unanimously, with another pencilled in before the year is out — and the curve read it as credibility rather than panic: the two-year went to its highest since 2024 at 4.72% while the ten-year barely moved at 5.00%. Traders now fully price three more hikes by the middle of next year. Chair Warsh, who again published no dot plot, blamed the bond market on growth, on capital expenditure competing for capital, and on geopolitics — in that order — and declined to be drawn on the President. Underneath the decision, though, the energy shock that forced it is quietly splitting in two. Brent futures fell 2.6% to $105.89 on a 7.1mn-barrel US crude build and Saudi Arabia’s plan to restore half the East-West pipeline within days — while the EIA’s dated physical Brent series printed $130.80 on 15 September and China’s pump prices hit a record as the world’s largest importer comes, in one analyst’s phrase, “off its crash diet”. That gap is the most interesting number of the week. It is also why UK August CPI overshot the Bank’s own forecast at 3.1% entirely on motor fuel — up 23.0% year on year — while core sat still at 2.6%, the ten-year gilt fell eight basis points and the FTSE 250’s housebuilders put on seven to nine per cent. And in Nairobi, largely unnoticed, three judges voided a $1.9bn transaction that had already closed and handed 15% of Safaricom back to the Kenyan state — a reminder that the cheapest risk to ignore is the one sitting under the title deed rather than on the tape.

Section 5The four markets

Hong Kong unveils its first five-year plan, broadening the city’s identity beyond finance

SCMP / AP · 16 Sep

John Lee delivered the city’s first-ever five-year plan alongside the policy address, and said accountability would follow if targets are missed. A finance centre that formally reclassifies itself as something broader is telling you where it expects the next decade of mainland capital allocation to go — and the Hang Seng slipped 0.73% on the morning after.

US and China discuss cutting tariffs on American energy and agricultural goods before the leaders meet

Bloomberg · 16 Sep

Talks cover American energy and farm products ahead of next week’s Washington summit, and Beijing is weighing adding BYD to Xi’s CEO delegation despite a 100% US tariff on Chinese EVs. If China becomes a bid for US crude and LNG again, the physical oil market gets tighter, not looser — which is the second chain above, from the other end.

SK Hynix explores options after report of an Intel tie-up to make chips in the US

Bloomberg / Reuters · 16 Sep

Reuters reported the Korean firm may lease part of Intel’s long-planned Ohio facility or form a venture with Intel and major cloud firms; SK Hynix says no decision has been made. Intel rose 5.9% premarket and the KOSPI is up 0.43% to 6,747.03. Memory made in America is the single largest structural change available to the AI capex cycle.

EQT plans to spend $50bn in India by 2030, $30bn of it on data centres

Bloomberg · 16 Sep

Roughly $30bn to data centres including Adani Connex and $5bn to the renewables to power them, against $26bn invested in India since 1998. A buyout firm committing twice its thirty-year India book to one asset class in four years is underwriting data centres as infrastructure, not as property — which is a different fund structure, a different hold period and a different valuation method.

Chinese domestic fuel prices hit a record as Beijing comes ‘off its crash diet’

Semafor · 16 Sep

The third domestic price rise since the conflict began, still capped below what global prices would dictate, while the world’s largest crude importer resumes buying after months of cut imports. China suppressing its own pump price while restocking is a policy decision to import the inflation rather than the shortage — and it takes barrels out of everyone else’s market.

UK inflation rises to 3.1% in August, the highest since March

ONS · 16 Sep

Up from 2.9% in July, CPIH 3.3%. The largest upward contribution was transport, with motor fuel +23.0% y/y and petrol at its highest since November 2022. Core was unchanged at 2.6% and services unchanged at 3.4% — which is the whole argument for a hold, and the reason the gilt market rallied into a five-month inflation high.

Bank of England decides at noon; a hold at 3.75% is expected

Bank of England · 17 Sep

Trading Economics notes a rise remains “broadly priced for November, albeit with less conviction”. With core flat and the headline overshoot entirely imported fuel, the vote split is the only real information in today’s release — specifically how many members are prepared to treat a 23% fuel move as something a Bank Rate can address.

UK long bonds are struggling even as the government sells less of them

Bloomberg · 16 Sep

Thirty-year yields are at their highest since 1998 and within a whisker of 6%, despite the DMO deliberately shifting issuance away from long maturities since 2022. The supply lever has stopped working, which means the problem is demand — and a demand problem cannot be fixed at the Budget by changing the maturity mix.

FTSE 250 jumps 1.06% as housebuilders and construction lead

Hargreaves Lansdown · 16 Sep

The index closed at 24,070.20, with Costain +9.31%, Bellway +7.06% and Galliford Try +6.99%. Domestic cyclicals moving that hard on an inflation print is a rate-path trade, not an earnings trade — the market separating headline from core in public, on the day before it had to.

Owner of Mergermarket and Dealogic cuts more than a fifth of staff under creditor scrutiny

FT (exclusive) · 17 Sep

Ion’s largest unit has stepped up cost-cutting this year amid increasing scrutiny from creditors. The financial-data business that sells deal intelligence to private markets is itself a leveraged private-markets credit — a useful reminder when assessing counterparty and service-provider risk in a fund’s operating model.

‘Economic sabotage’: South Africa vows not to surrender to the US on domestic policy

Business Day · 17 Sep

Ronald Lamola calls on world leaders to unite against President Trump. Escalating rhetoric two months before municipal elections is a rand risk with a date on it, and it sits directly on top of an AGOA-and-tariff relationship that SA exporters cannot hedge.

Pan African Resources posts a record R1.58bn final dividend as realised gold rises 54.8%

SENS / Investec Pulse · 16 Sep

FY26 revenue more than doubled to $1.16bn; a 65c final dividend totalling R1.58bn and a buyback of up to R500m were approved. PAN rose 5.6% to R26.77. A mid-tier SA gold producer choosing dividends and buybacks over growth capex at a $4,350 gold price is a statement about where it thinks the cycle is.

Grindrod eyes more Maputo cargo as Transnet opens its network to private operators

Business Day · 17 Sep

Transnet has also advanced private-sector participation with an LNG tender. Grindrod rose 4.5% to R27.84 and presented at an investor conference the same morning. This is the domestic vehicle the Cape-diversion trade has never had — see the third chain above.

Retailers slug it out as the FMCG sector faces a middle-market squeeze

Business Day · 17 Sep

Truworths fell 4.39% to a new low, Shoprite 2.66% and Pepkor 2.18%, while Spar rose 5.05% and Woolworths 2.20%. Anthony Butler writes separately that rising food prices threaten stability (opinion). Shoprite’s own FY26 case rests on food inflation improving from an exceptionally low base — which is the same thing the consumer cannot afford.

Kenya’s High Court declares the 15% Safaricom sale to Vodacom unconstitutional

HapaKenya / Investec Pulse · 16 Sep

The KES 204.3bn sale is null and void and the shares are restored to the state; Vodacom (−1% to R154.40) and the Treasury are both appealing. The market priced a Kenyan political story. The first chain above argues it is a valuation story, and not only in Kenya.

Fed raises rates in a unanimous vote, its first increase since July 2023

Bloomberg · 16 Sep

A quarter point to 3.75–4.00%, with an additional hike pencilled in later this year, aimed at inflation fuelled in part by the energy crisis. Traders now fully price three more hikes by the middle of next year; two-year yields hit their highest since 2024 and the S&P 500 closed down 0.45% at 7,551.81.

Why Kevin Warsh again skipped the Fed’s dot plot

Bloomberg · 16 Sep

Warsh attributed higher bond yields to economic strength, to a capital-expenditure surge competing for capital, and to geopolitics, and said the increase reflected conditions rather than politics. A chair who withholds the dot plot is deliberately removing the market’s favourite forward-guidance crutch — which raises the information value of every press conference and the volatility around it.

Senate blocks the CLARITY Act; bitcoin hits a four-week low

WSJ / Bloomberg · 15–16 Sep

Senators from both parties blocked the landmark digital-asset bill; Coinbase fell 10% and Circle sank with it. Bitcoin was $75,477 and ether $2,379 on Sharenet’s 19:54 spot. The industry was relying on statute to replace enforcement-led regulation; for now neither exists, which is a live classification and custody question for any digital-asset fund.

Foreign holdings of US Treasuries fell to a nine-month low in July

Bloomberg / US Treasury TIC · 16 Sep

Overseas holdings dropped $50.4bn from June to $9.25tn, led by France and Canada. Japan, the largest holder, fell $12.8bn to $1.1tn, and Tokyo is thought to have sold foreign securities to fund its record yen intervention. The marginal foreign buyer is now a seller for domestic reasons, which is the least price-sensitive kind.

Banks line up a $22bn chip loan tied to Blackstone and Alphabet

Bloomberg · 16 Sep

One of the largest financings yet assembled for the AI build-out. Separately, Goldman’s chief executive warned of higher costs and softness in fixed income. When a capex cycle starts arriving as syndicated bank debt rather than as equity, the cycle has moved from the income statement to the balance sheet — and that is where it becomes a credit question.

Section 6Opportunity Radar

Situations worth research time, ranked by the size of the price move relative to the change in fundamentals, by durability, and by relevance to the four markets. Research ideas only — never recommendations.

The UK curve has just voted that this inflation is not the Bank’s problem

Horizon: days to weeks · Confidence: medium · United Kingdom

On a five-month high in headline CPI, the ten-year gilt fell 8bp to 5.312% and the FTSE 250 rose 1.06% with Costain +9.3%, Bellway +7.1% and Galliford Try +7.0%. That is not a market that thinks 3.1% is sticky; it is a market reading the composition — core at 2.6%, services at 3.4%, both unchanged, and the entire overshoot in motor fuel at +23.0%.

The research question is whether the separation holds. UK domestic cyclicals have spent two years being sold on every headline print regardless of composition. If the MPC’s vote split validates the distinction at noon, the repricing of rate-sensitive UK domestics has further to run than one session.

Wrong if: the vote split shows three or more members voting to hike, or the minutes lean on second-round effects from fuel into services.

Watch: the MPC vote split and the November-meeting pricing immediately after 12:00 BST — not the decision itself, which is already known.

South African gold has moved from a capex story to a cash-return story

Horizon: quarters · Confidence: medium · South Africa

Pan African Resources doubled FY26 revenue to $1.16bn on a realised gold price up 54.8%, and chose a record R1.58bn dividend plus a R500m buyback rather than a growth programme. Gold is $4,346–4,354 and the rand is behaving.

The durable question is whether this is the sector’s posture or one management team’s. SA gold has historically converted every price spike into shaft capital and given shareholders nothing. If the FY26 results season across the sector repeats the Pan African allocation choice, the re-rating argument is about capital discipline rather than about the gold price — which is a far more robust thesis and one that survives a lower gold price.

Wrong if: the gold price retreats and boards revert to growth capex, or safety stoppages take the production base out from under the cash flow.

Watch: the capital-allocation statements in the remaining SA gold FY results, and the ratio of declared distributions to operating cash flow across the sector.

The Cape-diversion trade finally has a domestic vehicle

Horizon: quarters to years · Confidence: low-medium · South Africa / logistics

Houthi control of Bab al-Mandeb keeps Asia–Europe cargo going round the Cape, and this is the first time in the cycle that South Africa has a mechanism to monetise it: Transnet’s network is opening to private operators and Grindrod (+4.5%) is pursuing more Maputo volume.

Everything here turns on execution rather than on geopolitics. The volume is not in question; the ability to charge for it is. This is a research idea about whether a state-reform programme converts into third-party earnings, dressed up as a shipping trade.

Wrong if: underwriters accept Houthi safe-passage assurances and traffic returns to Suez, or the private-participation programme slips again as it has repeatedly since 2023.

Watch: a named private operator with an allocated tonnage on a Transnet corridor; and separately, SA port call and bunker volumes.

Digital assets: the statutory framework that did not arrive

Horizon: weeks · Confidence: medium · USA / digital assets

The CLARITY Act failed a procedural vote with senators from both parties blocking it; Coinbase fell 10%, bitcoin went to a four-week low at $75,477 and ether to $2,379, and the Fed hiked into it the following day.

For anyone auditing or administering a digital-asset fund this is not a price event, it is a framework event. The industry had been carrying an implicit assumption that statutory classification was months away and that custody, segregation and safeguarding standards would follow it. Neither is now scheduled. The research question is what the absence of a framework does to the classification judgements, valuation hierarchy inputs and service-organisation reporting that fund files currently lean on.

Wrong if: a revised bill is brought back quickly with bipartisan sponsorship, which would make this a delay rather than a reversal.

Watch: whether the bill is re-tabled before the midterm recess, and whether the SEC and CFTC respond with enforcement or with rulemaking.

Private capital is now underwriting Indian data centres as infrastructure

Horizon: years · Confidence: low-medium · Asia / private markets

EQT plans $50bn in India by 2030 — roughly $30bn into data centres including Adani Connex and $5bn into the renewables to power them — against $26bn invested in India across the previous twenty-eight years.

The interesting part is the asset-class reclassification. Committing that much to single-tenant, power-hungry, long-lease digital assets means treating them as infrastructure: longer holds, lower target IRRs, project-finance leverage and availability-based revenue. That has direct consequences for how these vehicles are structured in Jersey and Luxembourg, how they are valued, and what a fund auditor is asked to accept as a discount rate.

Wrong if: the AI capex cycle slows enough that hyperscaler offtake commitments shorten, in which case the leases behave like property again and the infrastructure framing is wrong.

Watch: the fund structures these commitments actually land in — infrastructure vehicles or buyout funds — and the stated hold periods.

Section 7 — desk lingoSound like the desk

Terms a PM would actually use on today’s tape.

Recap. Yesterday we did the crack spread — the refining margin that belongs to the refiner and moves independently of crude, which is why a country that closes its refineries imports somebody else’s margin; the pain trade — the direction in which the crowd is least prepared, which is where a thirteen-to-one flow imbalance eventually goes; and hedged yield — what a foreign bond really pays once the currency hedge is removed, and how it collapses without the bond moving at all. All three earned their keep overnight: the first in a physical barrel twenty-five dollars above the paper one, the second in a 94% consensus that made nobody any money, and the third in foreign Treasury holdings falling to a nine-month low.

Section 8Top 3 reads

How AI is transforming warfare

Financial Times visual investigation, 17 September

Target identification in Ukraine has gone from twenty minutes to under two, and the compute to run the vision models now costs about $250. Worth ten minutes because it is the clearest available account of AI moving from a capex narrative into a deployed, cost-curve-driven technology with measurable output — which is precisely the question hanging over every AI valuation you will be asked about. It also pairs uncomfortably well with the same morning’s news that Microsoft’s AI chief is publicly warning a rival about model welfare.

Hedge Funds Are the Wild Card in the Turbulent Bond Market

Wall Street Journal, 15 September

Read alongside the companion piece on hedge funds facing scrutiny over their growing Treasury footprint. With the two-year at its highest since 2024 and foreign official holdings at a nine-month low, the marginal holder of US duration is increasingly a leveraged relative-value fund. For an auditor of leveraged funds, the interesting content is the mechanics of the basis trade and its margin sensitivity — the thing that turns an orderly repricing into a liquidity event.

Americans Have Spent $100 Billion More on Fuel During the Iran War

Wall Street Journal, 16 September

The single best-quantified account of the shock that produced yesterday’s hike, and the most useful thing you can carry into a client conversation this week: a number, a period and a mechanism, rather than an adjective. It is also the demand-side mirror of the physical-versus-paper problem in the second chain — households pay the physical price, not the screen.
