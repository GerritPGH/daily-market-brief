Section 1What matters today

The Gulf war has reached Saudi oil fields. A Houthi drone attack on Saudi Aramco’s Shaybah field killed two people and the US ordered some staff out of the kingdom (FT, 21/10); Brent is $110.82, up 1.84%, at 05:30 (FT). Asia gave back Wednesday’s AI rally: the Hang Seng was down 2.87% and the Nikkei 2.03% at 05:30 (FT), with the New York Times reporting US and Israeli plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the midterms (investingLive). Wall Street had closed higher, the S&P 500 up 0.58% to 7,757.17 on SpaceX’s results and talk of a SpaceX–Tesla merger (Kiplinger; Motley Fool). Two inflation prints split the morning. UK CPI rose to 3.5% in September, its highest since October 2025, with motor fuels up 29.8% on the year (ONS via Investing.com), so a Bank of England hike on 5 November is now in play. South Africa’s CPI came in softer at 4.5% against 4.7% expected (The Pulse). Its medium-term budget delivered a smaller windfall than hoped. The deficit is 4.3% of GDP rather than the 3.5% some had pencilled in, and tax revenue was revised up R17.1bn, not R50bn (Daily Maverick; businessexplainer). The ten-year bond rallied anyway, to 8.71% (Trading Economics, single source). The rand closed at 16.36 (Sharenet).

Asia AI rally reversed; Gulf risk back

The Hang Seng and Nikkei fell 2–3% this morning after Wednesday's gains (FT). The Bank of Korea held at 3.00%, and a hike to 3.25% in November is seen as likely (investingLive). United Kingdom CPI 3.5%: a November hike is live

CPI rose to 3.5% from 3.1%, services to 3.7% and core to 2.8% (ONS via Investing.com). Borrowing is falling, but more slowly than planned, ahead of the 28 October Budget (WSJ). An FT report says plans to exempt mid-sized companies from audit could cost accounting firms up to £1.2bn a year in fees.

South Africa Smaller windfall, bonds rally anyway

The MTBPS cut 2026 growth to 1.2% and the deficit to 4.3%, and cut the fuel levy from 4 November (Daily Maverick). Softer CPI (4.5%) eases the pressure on the SARB (The Pulse; Business Day). The JSE fell 0.67%. Naspers (−3.50%) and Prosus hit new lows and Standard Bank fell 4.97%, while Sasol rose 3.83% to a new high (Sharenet).

ALSI 109,658 −0.67% · USD/ZAR 16.36 −0.11% · SA 10y 8.71% −18bp

United States SpaceX–Tesla lifts the AI trade

Stocks rose on AI optimism. SpaceX gained 7.8% and Tesla 3.4% after both boards formed committees to study a merger (Kiplinger). After the close Meta beat and raised the low end of its capex guide to $135–145bn (Bloomberg). Private investors now own about 70% of US government debt, the most in some 35 years (Bloomberg).
