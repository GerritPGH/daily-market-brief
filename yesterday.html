Section 1What matters today

Shares and bonds are pulling in opposite directions, and both set records on Monday. The Nasdaq 100 rose 0.87% to close above 31,000 for the first time, and the S&P 500 gained 0.66% to 7,773.99 (TheStreet; FT, 5/10). The ten-year Treasury yield closed at 5.315%, its highest since 2002, and the thirty-year touched 5.70% (Bloomberg). The trigger was the ISM services survey: activity eased to 54.9, but its prices-paid index rose to 74.0, the highest since July 2022 (FXStreet). John Authers calls it a top-heavy rally that is ‘daring bond yields to break it’: the S&P is up 22.7% since late March, yet 199 of its members have fallen (opinion). Japan’s thirty-year yield hit a record 4.23% before Prime Minister Takaichi told investors to ‘rest assured’ and promised to control new bond issuance (FT). In Europe, Spain called an election for 29 November, and the governor of the Banque de France said his country risks being ‘strangled by interest rates’ (FT). Saudi Aramco surprised the oil market by cutting its November price to Asia to $5 a barrel below the benchmark when traders expected a $5 rise (The National); Brent is at $100.53. South Africa’s petrol price is confirmed at a record R30.25 a litre from tomorrow. Brazil’s real gained more than 4% after Sunday’s first-round vote. Today, Bank of Japan governor Ueda speaks at 07:35 BST and the US trade balance is due at 13:30. The Fed’s minutes follow tomorrow, and the Treasury sells ten-year notes tomorrow and thirty-year bonds on Thursday.
