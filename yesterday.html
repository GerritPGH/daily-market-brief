Section 1What matters today

Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as chair did not change a rate. It changed the rules. He called the 2% target “a firm, fixed target” that is “not self-executing” — and, in the part that will outlive the week, he criticised the routine use of forward guidance and argued the Fed should preserve flexibility rather than pre-commit. Traders moved September hike odds from roughly 35% to 57% and sold the two-year 13bp to 4.36%, a one-month high, while the ten-year gave up only 4bp: a clean bear flattener, and the first time in this cycle the market has priced a hike as the base case. Then the weekend added oil. US forces struck Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island — the first attack in weeks — and Brent is back through ninety at $90.27, +2.46%, even as Washington signed itself into majority control of more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan reserves to refill the strategic reserve. Asia paid for both this morning: Korea's chip complex led the region down, SK Hynix −3.5%, Samsung −2.5%, Murata −4.1%, with China's August manufacturing PMI at 49.8 — better than July's 49.2, still under fifty. South Africa is already carrying the oil bill: R56bn more spent on fuel since the Hormuz disruptions, per this morning's Business Day, with another pump increase due on Wednesday — and that lands on a JSE that closed Friday at a record 118,173 with palladium up 6.5% in a session. London is shut for the summer bank holiday, so today's tape is Asia's, then America's. Watch the two-year, not the ten.
