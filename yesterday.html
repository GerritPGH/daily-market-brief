Section 1What matters today

The bond market has stopped listening to good inflation news. US core PCE inflation came in at 3.0% year on year against 3.3% expected (Benzinga, 30/9), and bets on an October Fed hike faded. The thirty-year Treasury still climbed to 5.64%, its highest since 2002 (Bloomberg, 30/9). Treasuries have just had their worst month in four years, with the ten-year up more than half a point in September to about 5.3% (FT, 1/10). When yields rise on soft inflation, the cause is supply and term premium, not prices. One possible seller has now appeared in the data. Japanese investors sold a net ¥1.9tn of foreign bonds in the week after the BoJ hike and another ¥0.7tn the week after (MoF via Trading Economics). Tokyo is up 2.7% this morning on the sixth straight improvement in the Tankan survey of big manufacturers and a weaker yen at 157.9. Micron beat and guided above forecasts, and Korea’s September exports hit a record $120.9bn, yet Seoul opened lower because of yields. In Johannesburg the All Share fell 0.85%: MTN dropped 7.2% and Tiger Brands hit another low. The rand is at 16.44, its weakest since early August. Brent has rolled to the December contract at $98.
