Section 1What matters today

The central-bank week is over and the weekend changed the questions. On Friday the S&P 500 finished +0.17% at 7,650.50 on triple-witching expiry while the ten-year Treasury went back to 5.00% (+6bp) (TheStreet, 18/9; Trading Economics, 18/9); London had a worse day, the FTSE 100 −1.45% to 10,659, and Johannesburg’s resources index fell 2.52% (Sharenet, 18/9). Overnight three things moved. Germany voted, and Chancellor Merz’s CDU took 4.9% in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and fell to second in Berlin, where Die Linke — which promises to take roughly 220,000 flats from corporate landlords, about 130,000 of them Vonovia’s — came first on an estimated 25.7% (FT FirstFT, 21/9; Irish Times, 20/9). The US and China opened trade talks in New York ahead of Thursday’s Trump–Xi summit, and EM stocks and currencies rose (Bloomberg, 21/9). And oil split in two: Brent futures slipped to $101.61 (FT panel, 21/9 05:30) while US diesel topped $6.50 a gallon, Saudi Aramco told European refiners they will get no term crude in October, and moving a cargo from Houston to Asia now costs about $26 a barrel (Bloomberg, 18–21/9). Two things decide the week for your markets: Tokyo is shut until Thursday with the yen at 156.93 after a hike that did not help it, and the SARB meets on Wednesday with the market pricing a hike at roughly a coin flip (Newsquawk, 21/9) and Morgan Stanley calling for one (Bloomberg, 21/9).
