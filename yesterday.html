Section 1What matters today

Three central banks decide in seventy-two hours this week, and the most consequential number behind all three of them is an American phone bill. US wireless telephony charges rose in August at the fastest rate since 2021 and, on Inflation Insights’ arithmetic, that single line item added 10 basis points to core CPI — enough to push the Fed’s supercore measure back to the upper bound of 3% and, with it, to settle Wednesday. Everything else in Friday’s print was better: headline 3.4% y/y, trimmed-mean CPI at its lowest since early 2021, sticky prices slowing, and the share of components running strongly above target falling sharply. It does not matter. Kevin Warsh told Jackson Hole he needed clear improvement to avoid raising, and “not worse” is not clear improvement, so the odds of a move on Wednesday sit around 90% and serious people are arguing for 50bp. The Bank of England follows on Thursday — a hold at 3.75% is priced at better than two-to-one, but the swaps market moved from two quarter-point rises over the next year to four inside a single week, and the FT reports the oil surge has reopened the argument outright. The Bank of Japan follows on Friday and is expected to go to 1.25%, with the ten-year JGB at 2.984%, 1.6 basis points from 3% for the first time since 1996. Markets, meanwhile, have decided all of this is good news: the S&P rose 0.86% on a hot inflation print and the VIX fell 11% to 15.84. Nobody is hedging Wednesday. The exception is oil, which is not cooperating: Brent fell 2.8% on Friday to $104.61 and then opened this morning at $108 after a Saudi pipeline closure, with US diesel at an all-time record near $6 a gallon. And in Asia the AI complex is already paying for the rates view — the KOSPI is down 2.43% today at 6,742, roughly 4% in two sessions, on the first day of its extended trading hours, with the Nikkei down for a third day. One quiet thing to carry into client conversations this week: the FT reports that between two-thirds and 90% of buyout funds that began deploying in 2019–21 will miss their target returns, on the estimate of five senior dealmakers at firms including Advent and Apollo.

UK amber

Still the highest benchmark yield of the four markets here — the ten-year gilt at 5.350% and the thirty-year at 5.91% — but the tape steadied into the weekend, with the FTSE 100 up 0.39% and the mid-cap index recovering off Thursday’s fourteen-month low. The argument has moved rather than resolved: markets price less than a one-in-three chance of a Bank of England rise on Thursday, yet the swaps curve went from two quarter-point rises over the next year to four in a week, and Deutsche Bank’s Sanjay Raja says “the case for staying on hold is weakening slowly”. The committee also decides the pace of balance-sheet run-off, six weeks before the 28 October Budget.

South Africa amber

USA red

Hot inflation, higher stocks, lower volatility — a combination that only makes sense if you read the print as settling an argument rather than revealing a problem. Headline CPI 3.4% y/y; the S&P rose 0.86% and the VIX fell 11% to 15.84. The ten-year still ended the week at 4.974% (Trading Economics), a three-year high and close to a nineteen-year high, and the thirty-year at 5.36%. Wednesday brings the first Fed rate rise in years, at roughly 90% odds, seven weeks before the midterms and directly against a president who wants cuts. Underneath, credit is where the strain shows: record convertible supply, high-yield spreads widening off a 270bp trough, and two hedge-fund blowups in one morning’s newsletter.
