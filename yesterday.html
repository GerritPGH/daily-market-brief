Section 1What matters today

Wall Street set a record on Tuesday, but only part of the market joined in. The S&P 500 rose 0.58% to close at 7,818.93, its first record close since August, and the Nasdaq 100 added 0.48% to 31,224.47 (TheStreet; Bloomberg, 6/10). The Russell 2000 index of smaller companies fell 0.57% on the same day (TheStreet). John Authers notes that value shares are at their lowest of the decade relative to growth shares, and that this year’s stock-market listings have raised almost $230bn, more than 45% above last year’s total (opinion). The ten-year Treasury yield closed 3.6 basis points lower at 5.275% (TheStreet) and stood at 5.307% at 05:30 BST today (FT). Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent promised again that growth and spending restraint will contain US debt; the bank Brown Brothers Harriman called its note on the speech ‘Bend It Like Bessent’ (Bloomberg). The market gives its answer at this evening’s ten-year auction (18:01 BST); the Fed’s September minutes follow at 19:00. Oil dipped below $100 on Tuesday and is back at $101.28 (Trading Economics, 7/10). Houthi forces attacked Saudi targets, and a tropical depression formed in the south-western Gulf of Mexico (Trading Economics; US National Hurricane Center, 6/10). In France, the extra yield the government pays over Germany fell from 141 to 127 basis points in two days after Marine Le Pen promised a legal limit on debt (Authers; FT). India raised its policy rate this morning for the first time since February 2023, by a quarter point to 5.50% (Trading Economics). In South Africa, petrol costs a record R30.25 a litre from today, and the Reserve Bank’s Monetary Policy Review defends early rate rises as ‘firefighting’ (Business Day, 7/10). The JSE gained 0.51% and the rand firmed to 16.53 to the dollar (Sharenet, 6/10).

Section 4Connections Engine

Two new chains today. The first runs from a failed monsoon in India to the price of the country’s largest flotation and the value of Indian assets held offshore. The second asks what happens to the dollar token that crypto funds use as cash if Washington decides it has carried Iranian money. Earlier chains reach their tests this week: Japan’s current-account and weekly bond-flow figures at 00:50 BST tonight, and the thirty-year Treasury auction on Thursday evening. These chains are hypotheses, not forecasts: structured speculation, labelled as such, with the weakest link stated.
