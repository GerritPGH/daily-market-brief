Section 1What matters today

The bond market did the talking, and it said one thing everywhere at once. The US ten-year yield jumped about 16 basis points to 5.13%, its highest since 2007, and the five-year closed above 5% for the first time since 2007, after the strongest US business survey since July 2021 (S&P Global composite PMI 58.4) and a weak five-year auction (Bloomberg, 23/9). Fed funds futures now put the end of the hiking cycle near 4.75%, against about 3% at the start of the year (Bloomberg, 24/9). It did not start in America. French ten-year yields rose 15.4bp and Italy’s and Greece’s nearly as much; gilts added about 15bp to 5.36%; and when Tokyo reopened this morning after three days shut, the ten-year JGB went straight to a 30-year high of 3.06% (FT, 24/9 05:30). Oil poured fuel on it: Iran’s president told the UN his country will not allow free navigation through Hormuz while sanctions and the US blockade stay, and Brent jumped 4.4% to $103.61 (Bloomberg, 23/9). Emerging-market currencies fell to their lowest since March. South Africa is where the day shows most clearly. August CPI came in below forecast at 4.4%, the Reserve Bank raised the repo rate 25bp to 7.25% unanimously and said it expects rates to stay broadly stable this year, and the rand still weakened 1.3% to 16.38 while the JSE fell 1.6%. The rise in rates bought the rand nothing. The JSE is shut today for Heritage Day, so Friday’s open has two days of this to absorb.
