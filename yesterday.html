Section 1What matters today

A week that was meant to be about Nvidia’s numbers and Anthropic’s listing has become a test of whether the AI trade can still absorb supply. Nvidia ended last week −13%, its sixth straight weekly fall (GRIT), closing Friday at $162.85 (−2.01%, Motley Fool), and is down 32% from its 6 October peak, more than $1.8tn of value (Points of Return, opinion). Over the weekend Jensen Huang discussed a comeback with allies and Musk and Tim Sweeney backed a ‘Bring Jensen Back’ letter, while Treasury Secretary Bessent called for ‘a dramatic shake-up’ and an outside CEO (FT). Nvidia reports on Tuesday. Anthropic prices on Wednesday at $70–78 a share for 825m shares, up to $64.4bn at a $1.3–1.45tn valuation, and trades on Thursday as ANT; Nvidia and Alphabet are each pencilled in for up to $10bn of a $26bn placement (Yahoo Finance; Bloomberg). On Friday the S&P 500 fell 0.40% to 7,561.67 and the Nasdaq 100 1.22% (CappNotes), while the FTSE 100 rose 0.36% to a three-month high of 10,902.56 (Share Talk). Bitcoin touched $62,112 on Sunday (Bloomberg, via The Sunday Crypto Desk) and was $65,279 at 05:45 GMT (blockchain.info). This morning Japan reported that its economy shrank 0.1% annualised in the third quarter against a +0.2% consensus (Xinhua); the Nikkei was −2.24% at 59,771 at 05:30 GMT (FT), and BoJ hawk Hajime Takata still called for ‘nimble’ hikes (The Standard). The rand fixed 1.49% weaker at 16.9635 (ECB) as Metair lost 35% on Durban port delays (Sharenet). Two new chains: Durban’s terminal backlog as a car-export story, and Japan’s contraction as a case for yen intervention rather than rate hikes; plus copper, which settled on its confirm line.

South Africa Port crisis, rand weaker

The All Share fell 0.45% and banks 1.53% (Sharenet). Metair fell 35.02% after warning that Durban port backlogs near 20 days could cost 20–25% of last year’s EBITDA (Sharenet; The National); Woolworths hit a new low. The rand fixed 1.49% weaker at 16.9635 (ECB) and the ten-year rose 4bp to 8.68% (TE). The ANC ruled out a national stability pact with the DA (Daily Maverick). The SARB decides on Thursday.
