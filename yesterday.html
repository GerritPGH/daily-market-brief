Section 1What matters today

Wall Street stepped back from Tuesday’s record, and the bond market set the terms for the day. The ten-year Treasury yield reached 5.36% on Wednesday, its highest since 2002, before the US government sold $39bn of ten-year notes at 5.300% (Bloomberg; FT). Demand was strong: bids were 2.77 times the amount on offer, and the sale cleared 1.7 basis points below the yield the market was quoting at the deadline (TFTC; Trading Economics). The yield settled at 5.28% and was 5.294% at 05:30 BST today (Bloomberg; FT). The S&P 500 fell 0.22% to 7,801.77 and the Russell 2000 index of smaller companies lost 1.31% (TheStreet, 7/10). The Fed’s September minutes show that all 19 officials backed the rate rise and that most expect another by the end of the year (Federal Reserve; Bloomberg). The same minutes record that the Fed’s trading desk has paused its purchases of Treasury bills, and today’s first chain starts there. A $22bn thirty-year auction follows at 18:00 BST. Oil is back at $102.59 (Trading Economics, 8/10): Hurricane Isaias has shut about a quarter of Gulf of Mexico output, and the International Energy Agency will not add to the stocks it has already released (Trading Economics; FT). In Britain, the FT estimates that the Iran war has cut the Chancellor’s room against his fiscal rules from £23.6bn to about £12bn. The FTSE 100 fell 0.79% and the thirty-year gilt yield went above 6% during the day (FT; Share Talk). France gave back the week’s gains, and its ten-year premium over Germany is about 140 basis points again (Bloomberg). South Africa had the hardest day of the four markets: the JSE fell 1.63%, banks fell 2.82%, and the rand touched 16.70 to the dollar, its weakest since late July (Sharenet; Trading Economics). In Asia, Samsung reported a record quarterly operating profit of about 107.4 trillion won, but sales fell short of forecasts and Seoul is down for a third day (Samsung; Trading Economics). Mainland China reopened slightly higher after its holiday, and Tokyo’s exchange plans to remove almost 700 companies from the Topix index (FT). Bitcoin is at $83,007, down 2.8% on yesterday’s reading (Trading Economics, 8/10).
