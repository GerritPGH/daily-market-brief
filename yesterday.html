Section 1What matters today

The bond rout paused, and the thing that moved instead was the yen. Bank of Japan board member Hajime Takata left the door open to a larger hike and to back-to-back increases, and the yen went from 160.16 on Tuesday's fix to 157.76 this morning — a 1.5% move in two sessions, with traders now watching for intervention on top of policy (Bloomberg, 2–3 Sep). The ten-year JGB, which printed 3.01% yesterday for the first time since 1996, fell back to 2.963%, and Japan's thirty-year auction cleared smoothly with yields above 4% — demand better than the twelve-month average. Every other long end followed it down a little: US ten-year 4.782%, gilt 5.222%, thirty-year gilt 5.86%. Gold rebounded 1.37% to $4,426.30 after erasing its 2026 gain on Tuesday; Brent eased to $95.21 even as Iran struck Kuwait and Trump claimed control of Hormuz. The Fed's Beige Book showed activity up only modestly and ADP said private hiring slowed in August, which leaves Friday's payrolls to decide whether a hike on 16 September survives the week. And the most under-covered fact of the day is the one this brief has built its first chain on: the Dutch central bank moved 86 tonnes of gold, about €10bn, out of New York and Ottawa — and said the reason was not safety but the speed at which it could be sold.

Section 2World at a glance

Asia amber

A relief session bought with a stronger yen. Takata's hawkishness pulled the currency to 157.76 and pulled the ten-year JGB back to 2.963%; the thirty-year sale went well. The Nikkei recovered 0.39% of yesterday's 2.85% fall, the KOSPI jumped 1.59% on semiconductors, and China's Caixin services gauge beat with a surprise rebound while the renminbi hit a three-and-a-half-year high. Watch the GPIF: an unusual meeting has started allocation speculation.

Nikkei 64,575 (+0.39%) · KOSPI 6,666.85 (+1.59%) · JGB 10y 2.963% · USD/JPY 157.76

UK red

Gilts stabilised; the politics did not. Prime Minister Andy Burnham spent the day trying to calm the bond market as public-spending fears mounted, with the surge in borrowing costs having already halved the government's fiscal headroom (Bloomberg, 1 Sep) five weeks before the Budget. The thirty-year sits at 5.86%, the ten-year at 5.222%. Elsewhere: one in three firms backed by the UK's Covid loan fund is now insolvent, and the FCA is asking whether firms will be ready for T+1 in 2027.

FTSE 100 10,756.45 (−0.30%) · 10y gilt 5.222% · 30y gilt 5.86% · GBP/USD 1.3493

South Africa amber

A broad down day — 257 decliners to 111 advancers — with the All Share off 0.67%, but the bond and the rand both improved: the ten-year to 8.82% and USD/ZAR to 16.09. RMB/BER business confidence slipped in the third quarter, with firms pinning hopes on November's local elections for policy certainty. Sibanye closed up 8.0% on a 64% revenue jump and a 201c interim dividend — then received a section 189 strike notice after the bell. Aspen fell 7.35% on results despite reaching net cash.

USA amber

Stocks reclaimed some shine as the Treasury selloff paused: S&P 500 +0.46%, Nasdaq 100 +0.23%.
