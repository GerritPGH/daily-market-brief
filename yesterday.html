Section 1What matters today

The Huang bounce lasted one session. On Tuesday the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.99% to 26,214.17 and the S&P 500 0.50% to 7,201.09, its eighth loss in nine sessions, while the Dow rose 0.85% to close above 50,000 (CappNotes). Nvidia gave back 5.86%, chip stocks (SOXX) fell 8.27%, Micron fell 11.01% on an analyst’s memory-glut warning, and Anthropic closed at $81.90, below its $83 IPO price for the first time. The money did not leave the market; it moved. Health care rose 2.95% on Sanofi’s $41bn bid for Insmed, and the equal-weight S&P beat the index. Asia is now paying for both legs: KOSPI is down 5.67% this morning after rising 6.32% on Tuesday (TE), Taiwan fell 7.2% early (Points of Return) and the Nikkei is down 2.91% (FT, 05:30 GMT). Tariffs are the second shock: Washington is preparing Section 301 tariffs on 16 economies, including China, Japan, Korea and the EU (Bloomberg; FT). A December Fed hike is priced as near-certain (CME 95–96%), so today’s October PCE, Q3 GDP and Beige Book are the last inputs; the Bank of Korea is expected to hike tomorrow. Gold fell 2.64% to a 14-month low area and credit did the opposite of equities: the high-yield spread tightened 11bp to 3.56%. For South Africa: Prosus and Naspers report today, and the rating reviews are on Friday.

South Africa Resources down, retail up; ratings Friday

The All Share fell 0.20% to 106,216.57 and the Top 40 0.30%; Resources fell 1.20% with gold and platinum, while Retailers rose 1.92% (Sharenet). United States AI sold, everything else bought

The Nasdaq 100 fell 1.99% and the S&P 500 0.50%, but 346 S&P stocks rose and 154 fell, and the equal-weight ETF rose 0.54% (CappNotes). Technology fell 3.26%; every other sector bar industrials rose. Consumer confidence jumped to 103.3 against a 92.2 forecast. NY Fed’s Williams backed a December hike, Governor Waller has favoured a possible 50bp, and the 10-year eased to 4.90%.
