Section 1What matters today

Seoul is in free fall this morning. The KOSPI was down 6.08% at 5,996 by 10:40 in Seoul, and a sell-side ‘sidecar’ curb was triggered for the first time since 19 August, after China’s CXMT said it would unveil next-generation server DRAM within the year. SK hynix fell 8.54% and Samsung Electronics 6.79%; foreigners sold ₩1.81tn while local retail bought (Seoul Economic Daily, 13/10). Tokyo, back from its holiday, is 2.08% lower (FT panel, 05:39 BST). The chip selling began on Wall Street. On a quiet Columbus Day the S&P 500 slipped 0.06% to 7,807.24, but the SOXX chip ETF fell 2.60% (TheStreet; Kiplinger; stockanalysis.com), and Broadcom’s chief executive likened many AI investors to ‘casino gamblers’ (Bloomberg). Oil went the other way. Brent closed Monday at $106.65, up 1.8%, as Houthi missiles hit Riyadh’s airport for the fourth time in a week and Russia and Ukraine kept striking each other’s energy sites; it is $105.41 this morning (Trading Economics; FT panel). John Authers argues that Trump’s diesel deal now depends on the Houthis leaving Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea bypass alone (Bloomberg, opinion). The IMF has raised its 2026 world growth forecast to 3.1% and the UK’s to 1.3%, but it warns that advanced economies will borrow 5.2% of GDP this year, the most since 2021 (Euronews; FT, 13/10). The FTSE 100 rose 0.99% to 10,656.28 and the ten-year gilt is 5.388% before today’s long-gilt tender. The JSE added 0.22% and the rand is 16.53 (Sharenet; Trading Economics). Kazakhstan’s central bank, the fourth-largest gold buyer this year, says it has started selling. That is today’s first chain; India’s coal crunch is the second. JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo and Citigroup report today, and US CPI is out tomorrow.
