Section 1What matters today

The AI stress test widened on Monday and moved from Nvidia’s boardroom into policy. Washington moved to limit remote Chinese access to advanced chips and to require licences for data centres in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil that buy Nvidia chips (Bloomberg). The Nasdaq 100 fell 2.67% to 27,197.15 and the S&P 500 1.92% to 7,416.59, the third straight fall for both, and the VIX jumped 18.7% to 22.91 (CappNotes). Nvidia closed 4.12% lower; Micron fell 7.62% and Intel 7.02%. Tonight Nvidia reports its first results since Jensen Huang’s exit, against a $109bn revenue consensus (LSEG via Shattered). Anthropic prices on Wednesday at $70–78 and trades on Thursday, with the book about seven times covered (Bloomberg). Bonds did not panic. The US two-year fell 5bp to 4.83% while the thirty-year slipped only 1bp, and Brent settled 3.55% lower at $92.44 on 2027 surplus talk (CappNotes; Bloomberg). The dollar is at its strongest since April 2025 and gold at its lowest in more than a year (Points of Return, opinion). In Asia, KOSPI fell 3.59% and the Nikkei 2.42%, while India’s Nifty rose 0.58%. In South Africa the banks index fell 3.13%, with Absa, FirstRand and Standard Bank each down about 4% on the week’s highest turnover (Sharenet), as the rand pushed towards R17 on fears that the ANC will drop the DA in the metros (BusinessTech). The SARB decides on Thursday. This morning a leak of more than 15 million Deutsche Bank files went public, coordinated from South Africa (Financial Mail; FT). UK jobs data are due at 07:00 and October CPI tomorrow.

South Africa Banks sold, rand near R17

The All Share fell 0.77% and banks 3.13% on R27.1bn of turnover, the most in five sessions. Telkom rose 11.29% to a 12-month high on MTN’s takeover talks, and Ninety One fell about 8.5% after its interims (Sharenet). The rand weakened to R16.97 on fears that the ANC and ActionSA will drop the DA (BusinessTech). The ten-year yield is at 8.72% (TE). Mid-month data point to a petrol cut of 256–281c a litre in December (BusinessTech).
