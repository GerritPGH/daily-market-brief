Section 1What matters today

The war premium is leaking out of the oil price and almost nowhere else. Brent broke back below $100 after American and Iranian officials talked for hours at the United Nations — hours after Donald Trump had threatened from the General Assembly podium to “annihilate” Iran — and Tehran let it be known it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if military pressure eased (CNBC, 22/9). Saudi Arabia restarted its East-West pipeline; Washington floated a $10bn fund with Arab allies to bypass the strait altogether. And yet the cost of hiring a supertanker from the Gulf to Asia passed $1.2mn a day for the first time on record this morning (FT, 23/9), American diesel is at a record $6.51 a gallon with the White House now openly examining an export ban, and South Africa is still looking at the highest petrol and diesel prices in its history on 7 October. Cheaper crude is not the same thing as cheaper fuel, and the gap between the two is where most of today’s brief lives. Everything else arranged itself around that. The Nasdaq Composite closed at a record on Meta’s Muse agent while the S&P 500 finished exactly flat — Bloomberg’s morning line, “never have stocks owed so much to so few”, is the honest description of this tape. Britain discovered a £3.5bn hole in a single month’s borrowing and its banks spent the next day telling the FT they would leave if taxed for it. Tokyo has now been shut for three sessions and reopens on Thursday into a central-bank hike, a Wall Street record and a typhoon that killed at least nine people in its own commuter belt. But the day belongs to South Africa: August inflation at ten o’clock, the Reserve Bank at three, and a consensus that says 25 basis points when one large bank says fifty and the country’s fuel bill says neither is enough.
