Section 1What matters today

Anthropic got its pop; the market did not. The largest IPO on record opened at $111.50 and closed at $97.17, 17% above its $83 price, then slipped about 4% after hours (CappNotes; TE). Everything else fell. The S&P 500 dropped 2.02% to 7,175.83, its sixth straight loss and longest losing run since 2024, and the Nasdaq 100 fell 2.21% after erasing an early gain of almost 2.5% (CappNotes; Bloomberg). WSJ reports investors sold other AI names to buy Anthropic: Nvidia fell 3.30%, and Alphabet and Amazon, Anthropic’s two biggest holders, fell more than 3%. The VIX jumped 15.8% to 28.44. Asia is taking the hit this morning: the Nikkei is down 4.85%, KOSPI 4.39%, and Taiwan’s Taiex about 6%, heading for its worst week since 1990 despite new short-selling curbs (TE; WSJ). The South African Reserve Bank held at 7.25%, but only by four votes to three, with three members wanting a hike (Daily Maverick). The rand weakened to about R17.47 late on Gauteng coalition worries (Sharenet; Business Day). Gold fell again: Comex was $3,529.50 at 05:30 GMT, down 4.14% (FT). Houthi missiles damaged the Riyadh tower that houses Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund, yet Brent settled flat at $91.98. India is the exception: the Nifty closed at 25,514, its highest since February, on record foreign buying (Business Standard). Andy Burnham signs the UK–EU reset deal in Brussels today (FT).

Asia Worst week since 2020

Asia is selling hard this morning: the Nikkei is down 4.85% at 56,560, KOSPI 4.39% at 4,912 and the Hang Seng 2.14% (TE). Taiwan’s Taiex is down about 6% today, on course for its worst week since 1990, despite new curbs on short selling that took effect today (WSJ; Focus Taiwan). Japan’s October CPI rose to 2.3% from 2.1% (TE), and swaps price an 84% chance of a December BoJ hike (Bloomberg). India is the exception: the Nifty closed at 25,514 and the rupee is above 93 to the dollar (Business Standard).

United Kingdom Six-week low; EU deal today

The FTSE 100 fell 0.93% to 10,452.68, a six-week low, led by defensives: Unilever, National Grid, AstraZeneca and BAT all fell more than 2%. JD Sports cut its profit forecast to £700–750m, and Babcock rose 9.4% on a 13% profit increase (Reuters via Euronext; TE). The FTSE 250 fell 0.71%, its sixth straight decline (PA). Gilts edged up to 5.32% on the ten-year (TE). Burnham is expected to finalise the EU reset deal in Brussels today (FT).

FTSE 100 10,453 −0.93% · 10y 5.32% +3bp · GBP/USD 1.3151 −0.13%

South Africa SARB holds 4–3; rand slides

The SARB held at 7.25%, but three of seven members voted for a 25bp hike. Kganyago said ‘the direction of travel is very clear’ (Daily Maverick). The All Share fell 0.33% to 106,216. Sanlam fell 7.80% after an operational update, and Investec about 5.5% after its interims, while Implats rose 5.21% (Sharenet). The rand slid to R17.47 late on Gauteng coalition uncertainty (Sharenet; Business Day). The ten-year fell 7.5bp to 8.78% (TE).

ALSI 106,216 −0.33% · USD/ZAR 17.281 +0.34% · SA 10y 8.78% −7.5bp

United States Anthropic pops, market drops

The S&P 500 fell 2.02% to 7,175.83 and the Dow 2.19%; decliners outnumbered advancers 464 to 37 (CappNotes). AMD fell 7.72% after short seller Hunterbrook alleged China exposure. Nvidia fell 3.30% and has lost $669bn in six sessions, and California’s attorney general has subpoenaed it (MarketWatch; WSJ). Hackers leaked Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan customer data, and Trump fired his cyber director (WSJ; Reuters). CME prices a 96.4% chance of a December hike.
