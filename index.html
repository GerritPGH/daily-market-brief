Section 1What matters today

The AI trade came back while America was at the Thanksgiving table, and today it gets a half-day to prove it. With Wall Street shut on Thursday, London and Johannesburg took their cue from Wednesday’s intraday reversal: the FTSE 100 rose 1.75% to 10,609.30, led by Imperial Brands up 13% after Altria said it is considering a cash offer, with Japan Tobacco exploring a joint approach (Share Talk; Bloomberg). The JSE All Share rose 1.80% as Resources gained 3.96% (Sharenet). This morning Nasdaq 100 futures are up 2.8% (Bloomberg, 01:13 ET). The Nikkei closed up 1.91% at 58,599, and China’s State Council issued a rare midday statement backing its stock market (TE; Bloomberg). The macro has not changed: the Fed is set to hike again on 8–9 December despite the turmoil (FT), and futures price that at about 98% (CME FedWatch via Milk Road). Oil now has a third flashpoint. After Ras Tanura, the Houthis struck Saudi Arabia’s Jazan refinery on the Red Sea, and Iran says ships have hit mines in Hormuz; Brent is $97.28 (TE, this morning). The Bank of England’s Financial Stability Report told financial firms, for the first time, to prioritise upgrading their encryption against advances in AI and quantum computing (FT). South Africa has a big evening: Moody’s and S&P report, with S&P expected to upgrade to BB+ (BusinessTech), and the Constitutional Court rules on the NHI Act. The rand is heading for its worst November in 25 years, down about 5% (Bloomberg via BusinessTech), though the ECB fixed it firmer at R17.55 on Thursday. US stocks close early today, at 13:00 ET (18:00 UK).
