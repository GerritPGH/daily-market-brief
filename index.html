Section 1What matters today

Brazil voted against the market. Lula won Sunday’s run-off with 51.79% to Flávio Bolsonaro’s 48.21% (FT), against polls that had leaned to Bolsonaro. In thin Sunday-night trade, CME dollar-real futures rose about 10% and the November real contract fell about 9%; a Tokyo-listed Ibovespa ETF fell more than 11% (Rio Times, 26/10). Bolsonaro wants the result annulled (Points of Return). Oil is the other weight: Trump says it is nearing “decision time” on a deal with Tehran or renewed US attacks (Bloomberg, 25/10). December Brent settled +3.14% at $116.72 on Friday and is $114.55 at 05:30 (cappnotes; FT). On Friday stocks and bonds fell together: the S&P 500 lost 0.52% to 7,623.68, the Dow 1.23%, and the ten-year rose to 4.999% (cappnotes). Global credit spreads are at a 17-month high on AI fears (Bloomberg). Gold closed below $4,000, at $3,993.96 (Trading Economics). This is the quarter’s busiest week: the Fed and the UK Budget on Wednesday, the ECB on Thursday, the BoJ on Friday, with Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon in between. For Jersey: an FT exclusive finds Jersey and Guernsey are early winners from the global corporate-tax rules, because multinationals have not fled offshore hubs. Today’s chains: the Brazil chain dies on its own kill line; the real’s crash tests whether the rand trades as its proxy; and jet fuel meets Rolls-Royce’s flying-hours meter.
