Section 1What matters today

The AI rout reversed in a single session, and the reason was political. Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Monday, after President Trump pressed it over the weekend (FT). Nvidia rose 31.48%, its best day since 2003, and won back nearly $1tn of value (CappNotes). The S&P 500 rose 4.73% to 7,237.17 and the Nasdaq 100 6.86% to 26,746.10, the best day for both since April 2025. The VIX fell 25.83% to 26.62. Two things did not bounce. Rates: futures now price about 98% odds of a Fed hike on 9 December (CME via CappNotes), the 10-year was unchanged at 4.92%, and Wednesday’s October PCE is the last big print before the meeting. And the ‘anti-AI’ trades: India’s Nifty fell 1.75%, its sharpest fall since July, and KOSPI fell 3.31% before rebounding 6.98% this morning (TE). For South Africa, the week is about ratings: S&P and Moody’s both review SA on Friday, and Investec expects an S&P upgrade, while the rand sits near R17.50 on coalition risk. Authers calls Monday a bounce that ‘can’t hide the dead cats’ (opinion). Huang testifies to Congress on 1 and 3 December, and the investigations remain open.

United Kingdom Rebound; Compass heads for New York

The FTSE 100 rose 1.30% to 10,480.82 and the FTSE 250 rose 1.73% to 24,061.90 (Sharenet; Yahoo UK). The ten-year gilt eased to 5.205% (TE). Sterling eased to $1.3074 at the ECB fix.

South Africa Stocks up; rand and politics wait for Friday

The All Share rose 1.71% to 106,428.34 and the Top 40 1.90%. Resources led (+2.30%), while Momentum fell 8.01% and Astral 5.43% after results (Sharenet). The ECB fixed the rand at R17.51. Investec’s Bishop says it has lost close to 7% in two weeks on the dollar and coalition risk (BusinessTech). Johannesburg’s mayoral vote was pushed to Friday (Daily Maverick). Business Day warns that job cuts at McCarthy and Continental may be the ‘tip of the iceberg’.

United States Best day since April 2025; hike still priced

The S&P 500 rose 4.73% and the Nasdaq 100 6.86%; 417 S&P stocks rose and 83 fell, but the equal-weight ETF gained only 2.59% (CappNotes). Only energy and staples fell. Anthropic fell 1.43% against the tide. Rates did not join in: the 2-year and 10-year were unchanged at 4.63% and 4.92%, the dollar index rose 0.52%, and NY Fed President Williams said the Fed has ‘work to do’. Flash composite PMI fell to 54.6, the lowest since July.
