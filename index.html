Section 1What matters today

The chip sell-off went global overnight. Seoul’s KOSPI fell 8.13% to 5,515.76 by early afternoon, with Samsung Electronics down 10.1% and SK hynix down 11.4%, and both Korean markets tripped circuit breakers on the same day for the first time since 29 July (Korea Herald, 15/10). Tokyo’s Nikkei was down 5.11% at 64,283 at 05:30 BST (FT panel). The trigger was a hot US inflation print. Consumer prices rose 3.7% in the year to September against 3.6% expected; core inflation was 2.6% (Trading Economics). The S&P 500 fell 1.11% to 7,718.94 and the Nasdaq 100 fell 2.02%, the worst day since July (AP; Bloomberg). Yet the bond market did not panic. The ten-year Treasury yield jumped to 5.32%, then fell back to 5.18% as buyers stepped in (Authers, Bloomberg, opinion); it is 5.114% this morning (FT). Futures briefly priced an even chance of a Fed rise this month, then settled back. In the Gulf, Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia have killed at least 18 people in a week (Bloomberg), and Ukraine says it will resume strikes on Russian refineries (FT, 15/10). Brent is $104.40, down 1.57% this morning (FT). The rand weakened to 16.57 and the JSE fell 0.82% on miners. Today’s chains follow El Niño twice more: into fishmeal, and into palm oil. UK August GDP is out at 07:00 BST.
