Section 1What matters today

The AI sell-off turned into a rout on Friday. The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, its seventh straight loss, and is now in a correction after its worst week since April 2025 (−8.6%). The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.61% to 25,028.98 and touched bear-market territory during the session (CappNotes). Nvidia fell 14.01% to $125.53 after Reuters reported the SEC is examining its accounting. Anthropic gave back 8.50% on its first full day, to $88.91. The VIX closed at 35.89, its highest close since March (CappNotes). Treasuries and gold went opposite ways: the ten-year yield fell 12bp to 4.92%, while gold fell about 4.6% to a 14-month low. Gold is now more than 30% below its February peak, which shows investors selling havens to raise cash (Points of Return, opinion). The damage spread worldwide. The Nikkei fell 5.06%, KOSPI 5.21% and Taiwan’s Taiex 6.23%, a record 17.5% weekly loss. The JSE All Share fell 1.49% and the FTSE 100 had its worst week since March (−5.1%). This morning, Asian regulators are stepping in. China cuts its minimum margin requirement to 80% from 30 November, Korea is readying a market stabilisation fund it has not used since 2008, and Taiwan bans short sales of falling stocks (WSJ; Bloomberg). KOSPI is still down about 4% at a ten-month low (TE). US futures point to a rebound of 2.2% on the S&P and 3% on the Nasdaq 100 (FT). This is a short week: Tokyo is closed today, India on Tuesday and Wall Street on Thursday. The pressure point is the Fed. Kalshi gives a 25bp hike on 9 December a 90% chance, even as Bessent warns against overtightening.

Asia Regulators step in; Tokyo shut

Friday was brutal: the Nikkei fell 5.06% to 56,436.65 (SoftBank −14.48%), KOSPI 5.21% and the Taiex 6.23%, a record 17.5% weekly loss (CappNotes). This morning KOSPI is down 3.80% at 4,685, a ten-month low, and Shanghai is down 1.1% at 3,494, its lowest in more than a year (TE). That is despite China’s margin cut, Korea’s stabilisation fund and Taiwan’s short-sale ban. India is the outlier: the Nifty rose 2.61% on the week to 25,800.60 on record foreign inflows (Business Standard), though it is down 1.19% today (TE). Tokyo is closed for a holiday.

United Kingdom Worst week since March

The FTSE 100 fell 1.02% to 10,346.34 on Friday, a five-month low, and lost 5.1% on the week (Corfe Capital). Miners led the fall as copper slid: Anglo American fell 4% and Antofagasta 4.5% (TE). The FTSE 250 fell 2.23% to 23,651.73 (Yahoo UK). Ninety One fell 20% on the week after clients withdrew a net £4.2bn (Corfe Capital). Gilts rallied with Treasuries, and the ten-year is at 5.24% (TE). The UK says the EU reset deal is agreed (FT). The CBI conference is today, and the Bank of England’s Financial Stability Report is on Thursday.

South Africa Rand near R17.50; metro pact

The All Share fell 1.49% to 104,638.77 and lost 6.9% on the week. Naspers fell 4.56% and Prosus 3.48%, both to new lows, and Richemont fell 3.97% (Sharenet). The ECB fixed the rand at R17.47. Investec says it is close to 7% weaker on the week as money leaves riskier markets. In Gauteng, the ANC, ActionSA, MK and EFF have sealed a metro pact, with Herman Mashaba set to become Johannesburg’s mayor (Daily Maverick). One positive: Eskom has a surplus of about 4,274MW and is cutting load reduction and diesel use (Business Day).

United States Correction; futures bounce

The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39 and is now in a correction. The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.61% and the Composite is negative for 2026 (CappNotes). Nvidia fell 14.01%, Broadcom 12.19% and CoreWeave 12.02%, and only consumer staples rose. Fed officials were hawkish: Cook warned the Fed could fall behind the curve, and Paulson is open to back-to-back hikes. Yet the ten-year yield fell 12bp to 4.92% as money fled to bonds. Futures are up 2.2% this morning (FT). CrowdStrike is in talks to buy Zscaler (Bloomberg).
