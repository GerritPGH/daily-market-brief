Section 1What matters today

Jensen Huang is out. Nvidia’s board ousted him on Thursday after reports of several affairs, one with a woman US authorities suspect of spying for China, and named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO (Bloomberg; FT). The shares fell as much as 14.3%, then closed −4.3%, still erasing more than $180bn of value (FT; Bloomberg), while AMD rose 5.86% and Broadcom 5.54% (CappNotes). The index barely noticed: the Nasdaq 100 slipped 0.30% to 28,289.19 and the S&P 500 0.35% (CappNotes). The bigger move was in rates and oil. A December Fed hike is now fully priced (CME FedWatch via CappNotes); the US two-year rose about 15bp to 4.88% and the ten-year 12bp to 5.10%. Brent settled 3.30% higher at $95.82 after the Houthis claimed strikes on Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline and Yanbu (unverified; Xinhua) and Trump said the US does not ‘need a deal with Iran’ (FT). Gold fell 2.79% to $3,876 (TE). In the UK, third-quarter GDP grew 0.7%, the fastest since early 2024, with about a fifth of it AI-related (ONS; City AM). And MSCI will drop Strategy, Metaplanet and Jersey-incorporated Yellow Cake from its indexes on 30 November. Today: US producer prices, the IEA oil report and Richemont’s results. Two new chains: MSCI’s rule as a break in the bitcoin-treasury flywheel, and Nvidia without Huang as a lender that stops lending; plus a copper chain that settles at tonight’s close.
