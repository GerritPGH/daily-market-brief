Section 1What matters today

Two clean prints and two decisions. Wall Street rose on the AI trade: the S&P 500 added 0.60% to 7,722.60 and the Nasdaq 100 1.29% (TheStreet; Bloomberg), and Tuesday’s $70bn five-year Treasury auction stopped at 4.845% with only a 0.4bp tail and dealers left holding 10.56% (Helious; Newsquawk) — the price-sensitive-buyer chain we ran last week dies on its own kill line. Seoul is the loudest tape in the world: SK Hynix’s profit rose about sevenfold (Bloomberg), the KOSPI closed +3.09% at 5,638.34 (TE) and is about 6% higher again this morning, tripping a buy-side ‘sidecar’ for a second day (TradingKey). London lagged: the FTSE 100 fell 0.73% as HSBC dropped about 7.5% on a profit miss (TE; Share Talk). Brent settled at $113.88, −1.67% (TE), as Israel’s election produced a deadlock and US–Iran diplomacy continues (FT; Bloomberg). Today, Chancellor Healey presents his first Budget — Newsquawk expects OBR headroom of about £15bn and a bank surcharge rise from 3% to 8% — and the Fed decides tonight, where markets price only a 7% chance of a hike (Points of Return, opinion). Our chains: the Budget settles two ledger theses today; the auction chain is closed; and a new chain follows Mercedes’ margin cut to its export plant in East London.
