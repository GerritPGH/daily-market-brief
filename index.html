Section 1What matters today

A quiet Budget, a split Fed and a split screen in Big Tech. Chancellor Healey left the OBR’s headroom at £14.5bn, down from £23.6bn in March, and raised the bank surcharge to 6% from 3% for 15 months, less than the 8% lenders had braced for (Newsquawk; Bloomberg). Gilts barely moved: the ten-year closed at 5.325%, −2.8bp (TE). The Fed held at 3.75–4.00%, but two officials, Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack, voted for a hike, and Kevin Warsh refused to give guidance (Fed; Points of Return, opinion). After the bell, Alphabet fell about 9% as a second quarter of negative free cash flow met $51.4bn of capex, while Microsoft rose about 4% on Azure +48% (StockTitan; Bloomberg). Seoul reversed: the KOSPI closed +6.63% at 6,012.44 on Wednesday, then fell 2.55% this morning as Samsung slid despite a record ₩107.4tn quarterly profit (TE; Samsung; Seoul Economic Daily). Brent settled at $115.32 (TE) as Israel’s count left Netanyahu short of a majority and the US told ships to avoid Hormuz (Bloomberg). Our chains: the Budget fades one thesis and half-confirms another; foreign money poured into Tokyo, killing a Japan chain; and two new chains follow €100 gas into fertiliser, and a tokenisation snub into ether.
