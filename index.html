Section 1What matters today

Nvidia passed its first test without Jensen Huang, and the market barely clapped. Third-quarter revenue was $115.8bn, up 103% on the year, and the company guided to $130bn for the current quarter (Nvidia). The outlook assumes no data-centre revenue from China. The shares rose about 3–4% after hours, having closed 0.44% lower (CappNotes; FT). The board says Huang will not return, even though he has offered to (FT). Before the print, the tape was weak. The S&P 500 fell 1.03% to 7,340.53, the Dow lost 848 points and the VIX rose 11% to 25.46. Software led the losses, with Salesforce down 9.45% (CappNotes). St Louis Fed president Musalem said several rate rises may be needed before spring (WSJ), and gold fell through $3,800 this morning (Moneyweb; TE). The new US chip-licence rule has started to bite in South-East Asia. Five banks turned down a $480m GPU loan for a Malaysian data centre built for ByteDance (Bloomberg via Seoul Economic Daily), and Malaysian data-centre shares fell by up to 20% (Reuters). Anthropic prices its IPO tonight, at or above the top of its $70–78 range and with a $90.4bn raise, and trades on Thursday (Bloomberg). In the Gulf, a projectile hit an LPG tanker in the Strait of Hormuz and its crew abandoned ship (Baird Maritime), after Iran declared civilian infrastructure near US bases “dual-use” (Bloomberg). At home, the All Share fell 0.82% and Sasol 7.76% (Sharenet); the SARB decides tomorrow. UK CPI is out at 07:00.

United States Fourth down day; Nvidia beat

The S&P 500 fell 1.03% and the Dow 1.64%, while the Nasdaq 100 lost only 0.72% (CappNotes). Software sold off: Salesforce −9.45%, Intuit −7.17%, ServiceNow −6.51%. Two-year yields fell 3bp to 4.80% even as Musalem talked of more rate rises. Import prices rose 1.1% in October against 0.7% expected (WSJ).
