Section 1What matters today

Big Tech’s bill came due. Alphabet fell about 13% on Thursday, wiping a record ~$500bn of value, after free cash flow of −$9.1bn and newly disclosed contingent commitments of about $98.5bn, including guarantees on data-centre leases (FT; Investec; Bloomberg). After the bell Intel fell about 22% on a $15–16bn guide, blaming a memory squeeze it expects to shrink the PC market 12% this year, and Amazon slipped about 5% on a weak Christmas outlook after more than $77bn of quarterly capex (FT; The Next Web; Bloomberg). The S&P 500 closed −0.30% at 7,720.39 and the Nasdaq 100 −1.38% (Motley Fool; FT). Overnight, Binance suspended all deposits and withdrawals after a breach it puts at $6.3bn, the largest crypto theft on record (FT; CoinDesk). The macro was hot: US GDP grew 3.4% annualised in the third quarter and core PCE rose to 3.1% year on year (BEA via StockTitan), yet Treasuries rallied, with the ten-year at 4.98% (cappnotes). The ECB held at 2.5% as Christine Lagarde said she will leave in February, and the BoJ held at 1.25% this morning (FT; TE). Seoul is about 4.6% lower today (TE, intraday). Our chains: El Niño gets its first confirmation in South African maize plantings while its gas leg dies, and two new chains follow the Binance freeze into ETFs and the memory squeeze into PC margins.
