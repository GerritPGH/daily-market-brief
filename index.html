Section 1What matters today

Two numbers framed Monday. Brazil’s real fell 8% to R$5.41 per dollar, its worst day since 1999, after Lula’s surprise re-election; an ETF of Brazilian stocks fell almost 25% before closing down 18% (FT; Points of Return, opinion). And Anthropic filed to list on Nasdaq as ANT: first-half revenue of $16.3bn against $1.2bn a year earlier, a net loss of $81bn that is mostly a $72bn revaluation of convertible notes, and 48% of revenue sold through the Amazon, Google and Microsoft clouds (FT; Bloomberg). Meta then said it will raise up to $95bn in shares. Markets took both calmly: the S&P 500 rose 0.70% to 7,676.80 (TheStreet), the FTSE 100 0.72% and the JSE All Share 1.03% (MarketScreener; Sharenet). The rand barely noticed Brazil — the ECB fixed it at 16.6291 per dollar, +0.19% — and Moody’s is talking about an SA upgrade within a year (Business Day). Brent settled at $115.81 (TE) and is $114.81 at 05:30 as Iran diplomacy picks up. Israel votes today; the UK Budget and the Fed decision are tomorrow. Today’s chains: Meta’s equity raise kills our credit-supply chain on its own kill line; a counter-chain asks whether Brazil’s loss is South Africa’s gain; and the real’s fall reaches the pulp mill.
