Section 1What matters today

The Gulf war reached Saudi Arabia’s oil headquarters overnight. Aramco’s head office in Dhahran was hit and set on fire in a suspected Houthi strike, after Saudi-led air strikes on Sana’a (FT, 14/10); the fire has been put out and Citi has told its Saudi staff to work from home (Bloomberg, 13/10). Iranian attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz have surged, with four ships hit on 11 October (Bloomberg, citing UK Maritime Trade Operations). Brent is $106.28, up 0.56% this morning (FT panel, 05:30 BST). Europe is short of gas just as winter starts: its storage is 73% full, the lowest for the time of year since at least 2011 (Bloomberg via Oilprice, 13/10), and the Dutch TTF price hit its highest since late 2022 this week (WSJ, 13/10); it is €85.99 this morning (Trading Economics). Wall Street was flat. The S&P 500 slipped 0.02% to 7,805.45 as the big banks reported strong trading and record net interest income, and their shares still fell; the KBW bank index lost 1.4% (Motley Fool; Bloomberg). The New York Fed’s John Williams says one more rate rise this year may be appropriate, but none in 2027 (WSJ; Bloomberg). China’s factory-gate prices rose 5.0% in September, the fastest since June 2022, and its exports jumped 36.3% (FXStreet; AP). Seoul is up 1.13% after Tuesday’s 5.47% fall (Trading Economics). In Johannesburg the All Share rose 0.60% and the rand is 16.46 to the dollar (Sharenet; Trading Economics). South Africa’s mines produced 12.7% less in August than a year ago, yet tax revenue is running ahead of budget before next week’s medium-term budget. That puzzle is today’s second chain; El Niño and Europe’s gas is the first. US consumer prices for September are out at 13:30 BST.
