Section 1What matters today

The market liked the deadlock, and oil sold off on the talk of peace. Once the Democratic sweep sank in, Wall Street had its best day since August: the Dow rose 3.03% to 53,356.12 and the S&P 500 1.58% to 7,688.62 (CappNotes; FT). Hyperscalers rallied while chipmakers and data-centre developers fell, after Texas elected a governor who wants a moratorium on data centres (Bloomberg). Brent settled 8.7% lower at $94.62, a two-month low, after Vance blamed the Iran war for the defeat (FT). Gilts rallied, with the ten-year down 11bp to 5.14%, and the FTSE 100 rose 1.79% (TE). Gold is back above $4,100 (TE; FT). This morning the Hang Seng is up 3.2% and KOSPI is down 4.2% (FT; TE). The Bank of England decides at 12:00 GMT. A 25bp rise to 4% is the consensus, and Goldman expects a 7–2 vote (investingLive). The JSE reopens today as votes are counted, after turnout passed 40% (Business Day). Our chains: today’s vote settles our oil-to-BoE chain. The fall in oil has killed our Rolls-Royce chain on its own kill line. And a new counter-chain asks why diesel is not following crude down, with US stocks at their lowest since 2003.
