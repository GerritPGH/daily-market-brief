Section 1What matters today

AI’s trouble has moved from shares into debt. On Friday the S&P 500 fell 1.09% to 7,659.44 and the Nasdaq 100 2.50% to 29,187.55, the worst week since June (TheStreet; Bloomberg). Chip stocks fell for an eighth day in a row, and the VIX rose from 16.46 to 19.99 (Cboe). The bigger signal came from the bond market. Debt backed by data-centre leases now trades well below face value: a Blue Owl–Meta vehicle is a little under 92 cents and Blackstone’s QTS “Project Excalibur” is below 88 cents (FT Asset Management, 19/10). CoreWeave’s 2031 bonds yield 15.78% (GRIT). This morning the FT reports that the Bank of England is examining whether Barclays traders misrepresented the risk of private-credit loans held through swaps. Oil added to the strain: Brent closed at $109.86, up 3.7%, after trading above $110 for the first time since May (Bloomberg). It is back at $107.87 this morning (FT, 05:30). Elsewhere the tone was firmer. The FTSE 100 gained 3.13% on the week to 10,881.90 (Share Talk). The JSE rose 0.69% as gold miners led (Sharenet). China’s third-quarter growth came in at 4.5%, against 4.4% expected (Trading Economics). France had its worst riots in years over the weekend. Wednesday brings UK inflation and South Africa’s medium-term budget. Today’s chains: a Barclays probe that could raise the cost of borrowing for private credit; a crowded bet against the euro that may already price in the riots; and a Japanese defence tax that could cool foreign buying of Tokyo shares.
