Section 1What matters today

The tape this morning is being written in Asian currencies and industrial metals, not in Washington. The yen has broken loose: through 155 for the first time since February and on to 153.52, a seven-month high, after July cash earnings rose 4.7% y/y — the fastest since 1997 — and Q2 GDP was revised up to +1.4% annualised, which turns the Bank of Japan’s October hike from a political argument into a data one (Bloomberg, Japan Times, 7–8 Sep). Bloomberg’s desk reports no clear driver for the move itself, which is the tell — a thin Labor Day session and a broken level did the rest. Korea went with it: the KOSPI added 2.45% to 7,166.90, now +119.8% year on year, while the National Pension Service quietly suspended its FX hedging as the won hit a two-year high (Reuters, 7 Sep). At the same time LME copper printed an all-time high of $14,415.50/t on expectations that Washington extends tariffs to refined metal, French and German ten-year yields sit at 18- and 15-year highs, and Brent is back at $98.05 with Iran saying a Hormuz shipping deal with Oman is days away while still threatening the vessels in it (Bloomberg, 7–8 Sep). Everything funnels into two dates: South African Q2 GDP this morning, where consensus is −0.1% q/q and would end the longest growth run in a decade, and US CPI on Friday, which decides a Fed meeting the market calls a coin flip.

Section 2World at a glance

Asia green

The strongest morning anywhere, and it is a currency story with an equity tail. The yen took out 155 and reached 153.52 — past the peak of the coordinated Japan–US intervention in August — on wages at a 29-year high and an upgraded GDP print. Korea’s KOSPI put on 2.45% to a fresh high while Seoul suspended the pension fund’s FX hedge to lean against a two-year-high won. Underneath, the split: China’s August exports rose 25% y/y and the surplus reached $119.1bn on AI-related shipments, but imports came in at +28.2% against 30% expected; Japan’s five-year JGB auction drew muted demand at 2.239%; China imposed anti-dumping measures on a Japanese chip chemical; and Nvidia supplier Wistron fell after announcing a $1.5bn global share sale.

UK amber

Rhetoric against arithmetic, again. Chancellor John Healey used his first big speech to call for “confidence about Britain” while conceding borrowing costs at a historic high — the thirty-year gilt is 5.82%, the ten-year 5.1525% — and the FT is now openly asking whether the Debt Management Office should take the Bank of England’s long gilts off its hands, which is the only lever anyone has actually identified. Jaguar Land Rover confirmed 4,000 job cuts over two years. Next won a landmark appeal overturning a £30m equal-pay ruling, repricing a contingent liability that sits across UK retail. And the FCA is asking whether firms will be ready for T+1 in 2027.

South Africa amber

The index and the economy have decoupled, and today is the day we find out by how much. Q2 GDP lands this morning with consensus at −0.1% q/q and +0.3% y/y against +0.5% and +1.9% last time; Bloomberg frames it as the end of the longest growth run in a decade and lays it at Iran’s door. Yet Monday’s JSE closed up: All Share +0.23%, Resource 10 +1.77%, Metals & Mining +1.90%, Sibanye +3.22%, Implats +2.65% — a rally powered by a copper record on a metal South Africa barely produces. Naspers and Prosus made new lows. Business Day leads on electricity and water constraining SA’s data-centre build-out; agribusiness confidence returned to positive territory in Q3.

USA amber

A market coming back from a long weekend into a week it cannot avoid. Cash was shut Monday for Labor Day, so the S&P is still at Friday’s 7,718.60 and futures are pointing marginally lower. The FOMC meets next week and is close to a toss-up: Kevin Warsh turned hawkish at Jackson Hole and August payrolls beat by a hundred thousand, but Governor Waller notes three-month core inflation has fallen from 4.76% in February to 3.05% through July. PPI Thursday, CPI Friday settle it. Meanwhile copper set a record on tariff expectations, the FT reports bankers pressing for top-tier credit ratings for Anthropic and OpenAI ahead of listing, and EverBank and WaFd agreed a $3.9bn reverse merger creating a $75bn bank.

Every figure dated and sourced. Where a source quotes its own live session change — the Asian indices, FX, commodities and crypto — that is the figure in the 1d column; where the instrument last traded before the weekend, the change is computed against the previous edition’s level in price-history.csv. Copper is quoted here in dollars per pound from Trading Economics; the record referred to throughout this brief is the LME three-month contract at $14,415.50 per tonne (Bloomberg, 7 Sep), which is a different contract in a different place — that difference is the whole of the second chain.
