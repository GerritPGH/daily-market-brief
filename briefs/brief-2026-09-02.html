Section 1What matters today

Yesterday the war got worse and gold went down. That is the most important fact on this page. American forces struck Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets for the second time in three days, Iran fired back, Brent is $95.29 — up 5.6% in two sessions — and gold has gone from $4,437.29 on Sunday night to $4,322.62, giving back essentially the whole of its 2026 gain (up just 0.7% year-to-date on 1 September, per mining.com). A war hedge that sells off during a war is not a hedge; it is a real-rates trade, and real rates are the story. Traders now put a Fed hike this month at about 70%. The ten-year JGB printed 3.01%, its first 3% since September 1996; the thirty-year gilt is 5.86%, the highest since 1998, five weeks before the October Budget; the US ten-year is 4.81% and the thirty-year 5.25%. Asia took the blow overnight — Nikkei −2.94%, KOSPI −3.46% after Korean inflation rebounded above 3%. South Africa looked quiet at −0.35%, but the ten-year is 8.84% and Business Day leads with Medupi's R42bn retrofit delay putting a World Bank loan at risk of early repayment. Watch what is not going up. When the classic hedges fail together, the problem is not the war.
