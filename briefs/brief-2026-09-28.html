Section 1What matters today

Diplomacy stalled over the weekend, and markets are pricing the cost this morning. On Saturday Trump rejected Iran’s seven-day plan to reopen Hormuz, though he told Axios he expects talks to resume this week (Bloomberg, 27/9). Brent is up 2.5% at $106.91 (FT, 28/9 05:30). Stalled talks mean high oil, and high oil means more Fed hikes. That combination is now hurting gold, which fell 2.4% to $4,183, a seven-week low; silver fell 4% and platinum 3% (Trading Economics, 28/9). The US ten-year starts the week at 5.20%, and the thirty-year closed Friday at 5.49% (FT 28/9; US Treasury 25/9). Stocks shrugged again: the S&P 500 rose 0.5% on Friday to 7,743. The markets reopening after Asian holidays did worse. The KOSPI fell 2.2%, with SK hynix down about 5%, and Shanghai fell 1.6% after a Trump–Xi summit that extended the truce by only two months. Two deals frame the week for South Africa and for private capital. First, Gold Fields made an A$38.7bn cash-and-share approach for Northern Star, which rejected it as “opportunistic” (Bloomberg, 27/9). Second, Blackstone is building a Lloyd’s vehicle with Aon that would join reinsurance placements at about a 5% discount to the lead underwriter’s price, and underwriters are furious (FT, 28/9; Insurance Business, 22/9). It is quarter-end on Wednesday, China shuts for Golden Week from Thursday, and US payrolls land on Friday.
