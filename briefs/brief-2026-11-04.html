Section 1What matters today

America voted for change, and the market voted against tech. Democrats won the Senate overnight, flipping seats from Texas and Kansas to Ohio and the Carolinas, and had gained eight House seats by the FT’s 05:35 count (FT). On the results, Nasdaq futures fell 2.01%, S&P futures 1.08% and the dollar index 0.55% (Yahoo ticker, 01:08 GMT). Asia took the hit in chips: KOSPI opened 2.4% higher, then reversed to −3.8% on fears of Democratic AI rules, led by Samsung and SK Hynix; the Nikkei is −2.63% on its return from holiday (Seoul Economic Daily; TE; FT). Before the vote Wall Street had bounced, S&P 500 +0.36% (AP), while London fell 1.14% (TE). South Africa votes today with the JSE shut, and the November fuel change is in: petrol −1c, diesel −R1.20, as a 48c slate-levy rise ate most of the Treasury’s relief (BusinessTech). Trump told the FT he has no immediate plan to strike Iran; Brent is $104.06 (FT). Our chains: the SA fuel family scores on today’s print, and two new chains ask whether Seoul is selling a regulation story into a memory shortage, and whether a weaker dollar and central-bank buying put gold back to work for SA miners.
