Section 1What matters today

Friday finished what Thursday began. The AI sell-off spread: the S&P 500 fell 1.82% to 7,580.07 and the Nasdaq 100 3.46%, leaving it about 9% below its peak (portfolio-terminal; MarketScreener; Authers). Intel fell 29%, its worst day in more than four decades, and the VIX closed at 19.42 (Bloomberg; Cboe). Gold broke below $4,000 to about $3,876, a one-year low on the futures, and took the JSE with it: the All Share fell 2.20% as gold miners lost about 6% and Eastern Platinum 31% (TE; WSJ; Sharenet). The credit side is showing strain: Partners Group’s US private-equity evergreen fund was asked to return about 15% of its assets and paid out a third of that, and a credit hedge fund sold $800m of debt on margin calls (PE Insights; WSJ). Binance reopened withdrawals on Sunday and about $3.6bn left within hours (Bloomberg). This morning Brent is down 3% at $109.82 on reports that Trump is pressing Israel to keep talking to Iran, and Opec+ kept December output unchanged (FT; Star-Advertiser). The week is loaded: US midterms on Tuesday, South Africa’s local elections on Wednesday, and a Bank of England rate rise to 4% expected on Thursday (FT). Our chains: the gold call and the sugar counter-chain settle in our favour, the South African trade-surplus chain dies on its own kill line, and two new chains follow the evergreen-fund redemption queue into junk spreads and China’s factory slump into copper.
