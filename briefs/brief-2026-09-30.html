Section 1What matters today

The oil shock is fading faster than the bond shock. JPMorgan estimates Middle East crude exports are back to 98% of pre-war levels (17.5m barrels a day), and Goldman agrees (Bloomberg, 30/9). The expiring November Brent contract is at $103.27, but December already trades near $98 (TE, 30/9). Bonds have not followed. The US ten-year is still 5.22% after the thirty-year touched its highest level since 2002 on Tuesday. That happened on the same day US consumer confidence fell to a 12-year low and job openings hit a five-month low (Bloomberg, 29/9). The dollar is heading for its best month since June, up 1.9%. Asset managers have built one of the largest short positions in Treasury futures since 2023, so a weak number could force a sharp reversal. The first test is US PCE inflation at 13:30 BST today; the forecast is +0.4% m/m (Investec). Micron reports tonight. Europe has its own problem: Vanguard calls France a ‘degrading credit’ and the OAT–Bund risk premium has reached 120bp. China’s factory PMI returned to growth at 50.1, but Beijing’s stimulus was smaller than markets wanted. Tokyo rose 1.9%. In Johannesburg the All Share recovered 0.31% and Gold Fields bounced 4.3%. The rand is at 16.39. South African drivers still face record fuel prices on 7 October: petrol is projected to rise by up to R3.12 a litre.

The Fed’s Williams said one more hike ‘late this year’ may be appropriate. Trump rejected new AI laws and backed independent AI audits instead. OpenAI is seeking $30bn at about a $1.4tn valuation.
