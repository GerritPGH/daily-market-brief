Section 1What matters today

New York bounced; the bill arrived in the bond market and in Seoul. On Monday chip stocks rose for the first time in ten sessions, the Nasdaq 100 gained 1.17% to 29,529 and the S&P 500 0.41% to 7,690.73 (Bloomberg; FT). But the ten-year Treasury yield rose 4.4bp to 5.142% (Kiplinger), and this morning Korea took the hit: the KOSPI fell as much as 4.8% on foreign selling and was down 3.64% at 5,557.87 by mid-session (Bloomberg via Bloomingbit; Trading Economics). It has lost about 17% in October, the worst of any major market (Seoul Economic Daily). The AI-credit fear is now being hedged directly: more than $9bn a week of credit default swaps on Oracle, SpaceX, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Broadcom and Nvidia traded in September, against less than $2bn in July (Morgan Stanley via FT, 20/10). In Europe, France's ten-year yield hit 4.933%, the highest since July 2002, and its spread over Bunds rose to 148bp, the widest since 2012 (Points of Return). London fell 1.08%, with Barclays down 3.26% on the Bank of England report (Share Talk), and the JSE fell 1.75% as Anglo American and Glencore lost more than 5% (Sharenet). Wednesday brings UK inflation and South Africa's medium-term budget, where Ramaphosa has signalled fuel relief (Daily Maverick). Today's two chains are both counter-chains: GPU rents are rising while CoreWeave's bonds price a bust; and an Indian ethanol decision that could free sugar for export.
