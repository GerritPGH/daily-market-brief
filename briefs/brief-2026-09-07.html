Section 1What matters today

Two hundred billion dollars of official money left the US Treasury market last week for reasons that have nothing to do with America. Japan’s foreign reserves fell a record $79.6bn in August to $1.208tn — and the securities line alone fell $87.8bn, because the Ministry of Finance funded a ¥15.4tn ($98.7bn) yen defence, the largest single month on record, by liquidating a reserve pot that market participants put at roughly 70% US Treasuries (Reuters, Bloomberg, 6–7 Sep). Three days earlier the manager of Norway’s $2.3tn fund proposed cutting its own Treasury book by about $80bn. Neither is a view on the United States; both are sales. Meanwhile Friday’s payrolls came in at +162,000 against a +58,000 consensus, which put a 16 September hike back on the table — the S&P fell 0.38%, the two-year went to 4.37% and CNBC this morning has the ten-year facing a 4.8% test. Add an oil market that will not settle — Iran and the US traded tanker strikes over the weekend, Iran is drawing an “exclusion zone” outside Hormuz, OPEC+ declined to add a barrel for October, and Brent is $97.40 — and a German state election in which the AfD took 44.5%. Asia this morning refused to care: KOSPI +3.40%, Nikkei +1.93%, Hang Seng −1.00%. US markets are closed today for Labor Day, so the tape you see is Asia’s and Europe’s alone.

Section 2World at a glance

Asia green

A two-speed morning, and the split is the information. Korea put on 3.40% — the largest single move anywhere — and Japan 1.93%, while Hong Kong fell 1.00% and India 0.51%. Tech led; oil-importing currencies did not (Bloomberg: the peso “left behind in Asia as oil shock erodes dollar reserves”). Underneath: Japan’s reserves posted their largest-ever monthly fall after a record intervention; Beijing is putting 360bn yuan ($54bn) into ICBC, AgBank, China Life and Eximbank, and the recipients’ shares fell; South Korea is merging five utilities to prepare for AI and chip power demand; Moonshot AI filed confidentially in Hong Kong at a reported $50bn; and a volcanic eruption has shut Jakarta’s airports for a second day.

UK amber

Better data, worse arithmetic. KPMG/REC reported UK hiring accelerating for the first time in four years, and the Chancellor will say today that the economy is “turning a corner” — against a thirty-year gilt still at 5.78% and a ten-year at 5.156%, and Bloomberg reporting gilts, BTPs and OATs hit together this morning by energy-shock and political risk. South Africa amber

The rand is holding but the oil bill is not. Brent at $97.40 has already produced fuel increases of R1.34/l on petrol and up to R3.15/l on diesel, and Investec’s Annabel Bishop now flags a possible 25bp SARB hike on 23 September — the last MPC vote was 4–2 with two members already wanting one (IOL, 4 Sep). The ten-year nevertheless firmed to 8.695%. Moneyweb reports SA listed property losing its shine as investors take profits; Icasa is opening an inquiry into pricing by the big telcos, Netflix and WhatsApp; Eskom faces an uphill fight to delay its transmission split past 2027; and Daily Maverick reports the grain industry heading into a challenging, dry summer.

USA red

Closed for Labor Day — which means Friday’s print sits unhedged for a full extra day. Payrolls at +162,000 beat consensus by more than a hundred thousand with unemployment steady at 4.1%; yields rose, stocks fell, and the President immediately called for a rate cut. Around that: the SEC has sued ISS as the administration widens its scrutiny of proxy advisers, the FT reports KPMG escalated a control finding at a Guggenheim asset-management subsidiary to a material weakness, and Reuters calls diesel — at a record — “the real spoiler for US inflation doves”.
