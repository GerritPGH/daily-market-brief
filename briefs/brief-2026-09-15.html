Section 1What matters today

The ten-year US Treasury note traded through 5% yesterday for the first time since 2023 — 5.01% at the high, back to 4.996% on the FT’s 05:30 panel this morning and 5.028% live on Trading Economics as Asia traded — and it did so on the eve of a Federal Reserve meeting that is expected to raise rates. Futures put the odds of a quarter-point rise tomorrow evening at about 85%, and Goldman’s Ben Snider notes the market already prices more than three hikes over the next year. The Bank of England follows on Thursday at 3.75%, the Bank of Japan on Friday, where a move to 1.25% would be the third rise in under ten months — the fastest sequence since 1990, when the asset bubble was bursting. Three decisions in seventy-two hours, and the thing forcing all three is a supply shock none of them can reach: Saudi Arabia shut its East-West crude pipeline on 11 September after drone attacks from Iraq and says it will be out of service for weeks, removing about 4% of global crude supply; the Iran–Gulf meeting on the Strait of Hormuz has been postponed; Houthi forces have taken Mocha, on the Bab al-Mandeb; and Brent is $107.21. Against that, the market’s other story is that the AI industry has begun arguing with itself — Anthropic’s Dario Amodei, then Microsoft with published rules limiting its own model development, then Altman and Musk in different words, called for a slower pace. A semiconductor gauge fell 5.9%; SoftBank −11%, SK Hynix −6.4%, Kioxia −6.4%, ASML −4.1%. Donald Trump called the risk a “HOAX”. Two numbers to carry into client conversations: Fitch put the US private-credit default rate at a record 6.3%, and India’s Solar Industries bid R21.8bn in cash for Omnia, taking one more mid-cap off the JSE.

Section 2World at a glance

Asia red

The AI complex took the worst of Monday and has only half-steadied. SoftBank fell 11% in Tokyo, memory makers 4–6.4%, and the KOSPI 3.3%; overnight the KOSPI is +0.07% at 6,688.73 and the Nikkei +0.31%, which is a pause rather than a recovery. The structural event is in rates: the ten-year JGB has gone through 3%, at 3.002%, for the first time since 1996, two days before a Bank of Japan meeting expected to hike to 1.25%. Meanwhile Kioxia is weighing a $10bn US listing and ByteDance has syndicated a $29.6bn loan — both very large Asian raisings routed away from domestic equity markets.

UK amber

The FTSE 100 was the only major index to rise — +0.44% to 10,698 — which is what an energy-and-defensives index does in an oil shock, and the FTSE 250 fell 0.59%, which is what the domestic economy actually looks like. The gilt is still the highest benchmark yield of the four markets here at 5.390%, with the thirty-year at 5.91%. The FT’s lead this morning is that the Iran war and the global bond selloff have pushed UK borrowing costs to levels not seen since before the 2008 crash, leaving Chancellor John Healey with very little room six weeks before the Budget. The MPC decides on Thursday.

USA red

A bad-news Monday: the ten-year through 5% intraday for the first time since 2023, the S&P down 0.50% and the Nasdaq 100 down 0.80% on the AI-slowdown call, a semiconductor gauge down 5.9%, and Bank of America down 5.1% after guiding to flat trading revenue.
