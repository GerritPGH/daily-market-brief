Section 1What matters today

The bond market kept going, and this time it reached the far end of the curve. The US thirty-year yield rose as much as seven basis points to 5.47%, its highest since 2004, and the ten-year is at 5.19% this morning (Bloomberg, 24/9; FT, 25/9 05:30). Oil did most of the work: Brent settled up about 4% at $107.19 on Thursday (Bloomberg, 24/9 16:45 ET) before easing to $105.62 overnight on a report that Washington and Tehran are exploring a phased deal to reopen Hormuz. Equities barely noticed; the S&P 500 closed flat at 7,704. The cracks are showing in the plumbing instead. Blackstone shelved a $3bn bond deal secured on old private-equity fund stakes because buyers balked at the leverage and the age of the assets (Private Equity Wire, 24/9), and Oracle sent a force majeure notice to Blue Owl over its 2.5GW New Mexico data-centre campus, so that it can delay rent if the project slips (DCD / Bloomberg, 24/9). Both are what a 5% risk-free rate does to structures built for 3%. In Asia, Hong Kong fell 1.8%, Tokyo rose for a fifth day, and the yen got political cover: Japan’s finance minister says Trump raised concerns about the weak yen with Prime Minister Takaichi, and Goldman now sees 150 within a year. The JSE reopens today after Heritage Day with two days of this to absorb, the rand at 16.42, and the new 7.25% repo rate in force from this morning.
