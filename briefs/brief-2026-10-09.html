Section 1What matters today

Thursday tested the two things holding this market up, and gave different answers. The first was demand for artificial intelligence. The FT reported that OpenAI’s annualised revenue was approaching $50bn at the end of September, about $20bn less than the $70bn reported last month (FT, 8/10). The Nasdaq 100 fell 1.39% to 30,725.81 and the S&P 500 0.47% to 7,765.36; Nvidia lost 2.9%, Micron 4.8% and Oracle 5.5% (Yahoo; FT). This morning the FT says SoftBank’s Masayoshi Son is seeking up to $100bn from Gulf investors for his AI bets, and SoftBank’s shares are down 7.1% in Tokyo (FT, 9/10; Trading Economics). The second was demand for long-dated government debt. The US sold $22bn of thirty-year bonds at 5.618%, with bids for 2.54 times the amount on offer against an average of 2.41 (Reuters, 8/10). The ten-year yield, which touched 5.35% in the morning, closed five basis points lower at 5.227% and was 5.223% at 05:34 BST today (Reuters; FT). Oil did its best to spoil that. Brent rose as much as 5.7%, past $105, after a tanker was hit off Qatar and a report that the White House had asked for options to strike Iran before the midterm elections; it settled at $103.92 and eased to $103.09 this morning after President Trump said the US ‘will not be attacking Iran’ before the vote (Bloomberg; Reuters; Trading Economics). Hurricane Isaias, with winds of 105 mph, is due on the northern Gulf coast late today or early on Saturday (National Hurricane Center). In Britain, Labour held Holborn and St Pancras, the ten-year gilt yield touched 5.51%, its highest since July 2007, and the FCA proposed that funds holding property must require 90 days’ notice of withdrawal, where today’s first chain begins (FT; Trading Economics; FCA). South Africa’s factory output fell 4.3% in August against a forecast rise, and the JSE finished five points higher at 107,109.29 (Stats SA, via IOL; Sharenet). In Asia, Singapore had its worst day since April 2025 on Thursday, down 3.5%, Seoul fell 2.62% and is closed today, and India’s rupee is within 0.2% of its record low (Bloomberg; Trading Economics). Gold is back at $4,201 and bitcoin is $81,875 (Trading Economics, 9/10).
