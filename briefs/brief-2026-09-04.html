Section 1What matters today

One dovish sentence from a Fed governor undid a week of the bond market's bad mood. Christopher Waller said he could back holding rates if price pressures keep easing — Reuters called it his “give disinflation a chance” speech — and the whole risk complex turned on it: the S&P 500 +1.06% to 7,747.71, the Nasdaq 100 +1.16%, the dollar to its weakest since May, and Asia this morning following hard (Nikkei +1.38%, Hang Seng +2.07%). Note what is being priced: this is a market relieved that the Fed might not hike on 16 September — CME FedWatch has had a quarter-point rise at roughly 60/40. Yields fell most at the front (US two-year 4.34%, −5bp) and least at the back (thirty-year 5.27%), and every long end in the world exhaled with it: gilt 5.17%, thirty-year gilt 5.784%, SA ten-year 8.74%, JGB 2.90%. The yen kept rallying on carry unwinding into the BoJ's 17–18 September meeting (156.31), gold made a new high at $4,491.70 and the PGMs ran hard, which handed Johannesburg a +1.40% day on the back of a very good Implats result. All of it is provisional until 13:30 BST, when August payrolls land with consensus at +58,000. And the story almost nobody is pricing: the manager of Norway's $2.3tn oil fund has proposed cutting its US Treasury book by about $80bn.

Section 2World at a glance

Asia green

A genuine risk rally, and an unusual one — equities up and the funding currency up. The yen strengthened again on carry-trade exodus ahead of the BoJ's 17–18 September meeting, where a move from 1.00% to 1.25% is now the base case, and Japan's ten-year fell to 2.90%. Korea led on semiconductors for a second session; Hong Kong put on 2.07% with mainland money rotating into tech and out of financials. Two structural items worth more than the tape: ByteDance raised a $37.6bn loan, Asia's second-largest of 2026, and the SFC reclassified authorised funds with 50%-plus private-markets exposure as complex products.

UK amber

The curve got a day off; the politics did not. Gilts rallied with everything else — ten-year 5.17%, thirty-year 5.784%, down about 8bp and off the 1998 high — but the pressure is unchanged five weeks before Prime Minister Andy Burnham's Budget. Two things landed against it. Trump's trade representative Jamieson Greer told the FT that Britain choosing “Brussels over America” is a “problem” for expanding the US trade deal. And the BoE's chief economist Huw Pill said out loud what the gilt market has been implying: that central banks are being pushed towards financing deficits. Governor Bailey speaks at the LSE today.

South Africa green

The best day on the JSE in weeks, and it was made of metal. The All Share rose 1.40% with Resource 10 up 2.66% and Metals & Mining up 3.23% as platinum and palladium ran; Implats +9.0% on FY sales up 58% to R135bn, R21bn of free cash flow and a R9.55 special dividend on top of a R4.90 ordinary. Discovery lifted normalised headline earnings 21% to R11.8bn; Aspen added 6.5%. The rand firmed to 15.99 and the ten-year fell 8bp to 8.74%. Against that: Trump signed a two-year Agoa extension, well short of the fifteen years Pretoria asked for, and the IDC's CFO resigned amid a R4.7bn loss.

USA amber

Waller did the work. Stocks and bonds rallied together on his hint that a hold is defensible, the dollar fell to a four-month low, and the front end led the move. The data underneath was more mixed than the tape: services activity expanded again in August but input prices rose, jobless claims ticked back up, and the July trade deficit widened to its largest in over a year — driven, per the Straits Times' read, by the AI build-out pulling in imported hardware. Single names told the consumer story: Lululemon −15%, Victoria's Secret disappointing, Adobe changing CEO. Payrolls at 13:30 BST decide the fortnight.
