Section 1What matters today

The oil shock stopped being an oil story yesterday and became a fiscal one, and three finance ministries were handed the same bill in three different currencies. In Washington, Scott Bessent tripled the Treasury’s long-dated buyback to $6bn to treat what he had called a “fever” in the bond market — and the market sold anyway, taking the ten-year to 4.847%, its highest since 2023, because it had expected more. Evercore ISI’s verdict is the sentence of the week: the announcement suggests he is “recognising that the US cannot durably prevent fundamentals from dictating yields”. In London the bill arrived as a curve: the ten-year gilt backed up 7.2bp to 5.262%, the largest one-day move of any major curve, on a day the shock was a commodity — because Britain funds more of its debt in inflation-linked form than any other G7 sovereign, so a barrel of Brent is a debt-service line item there before it is a policy question. And in Pretoria it arrived as a sentence no minister enjoys saying: South Africans must choose between record pump prices and higher taxes (Enoch Godongwana, via BusinessTech, 9 Sep). Underneath it all sits a number that is not a crude price at all: US retail diesel at $5.94 a gallon, an all-time record, above the post-Ukraine peak. That is a refining shortage, not a barrel shortage, and it is the thing that will reach a South African maize field in October. Brent $101.06, the FTSE at a seven-week low, the JSE’s banks down 1.89% beneath a flat-looking index, and platinum up 4.06%. Two events settle it: the ECB at 13:15, hiking as the only G7 central bank still tightening, and US PPI at 13:30, with consensus looking for 5.3% y/y against 4.7% — the first hard read on whether the shock has reached the producer. CPI follows tomorrow.

Section 2World at a glance

Asia red

Sold across the board with one loud exception. The Nikkei is 0.59% lower this morning and the Hang Seng 1.29%, but the KOSPI rose 1.40% to 7,052 on semiconductors — and Apple then unveiled a foldable iPhone at $1,999 rising to $3,199, an industry record, which is a bill of materials written largely in Korean won. The yen has now held its break for six sessions at 153.37 through Bessent’s “I am the house now”, and the JGB ten-year finally moved with it, up 5bp to 2.931%. China quietly restarted crude buying, removing one of the last lids on the oil price.

UK red

The worst combination available: equities down and yields up, on the same input. The FTSE 100 fell 1.31% to 10,670, a seven-week low, explicitly on Brent above $101 — an index roughly a fifth energy and materials, selling off on an energy rally, which tells you it was read as a demand tax. Meanwhile the ten-year gilt took the largest back-up of any major curve and the thirty-year sits at its record 5.82%. Continental equities were worse still (DAX −1.74%, CAC −1.94%). Elsewhere: Britain charges three times any other G7 country for citizenship, and the City is about to let companies put their logos on buildings for the first time in a century.

South Africa red

A quiet-looking index over a genuinely ugly interior. All Share −0.80%, but Banks −1.89%, Life Insurance −1.84% and Financial 15 −1.76%, with Standard Bank −4.18% and Discovery −3.10% — a rates-and-credit trade, on the day the SA ten-year backed up to 8.845% on inflation risk. Against that, Chemicals +7.78%, the largest sector move on the board by a factor of two, and Thungela +4.86%. The policy backdrop: Godongwana’s petrol-or-taxes framing, an AfDB R82bn emergency facility for African energy and fertiliser, and a Reserve Bank paper arguing the 3% target can absorb shocks. Growthpoint and Hyprop both delivered.

USA amber

The Treasury blinked and the market called it. A buyback tripled to $6bn was met with disappointment and a ten-year at 4.85%, the highest since 2023 — and yet the S&P lost only 0.48% and clawed back most of an intraday fall, because the AI capex cycle is still doing the work that ordinarily belongs to the discount rate. Trump then promised every adult citizen a $5,000 dividend if the GOP holds Congress, a pledge costing well over $1tn, floated the day after the Treasury admitted the limits of its own demand management. Diesel is at a record, PPI lands today and CPI tomorrow, and Absolute Strategy’s model says pump prices staying here for six months put headline inflation at 4.9%.
