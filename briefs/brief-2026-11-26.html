Section 1What matters today

Wall Street went to the Thanksgiving table after a day that nearly broke and then didn’t. The Nasdaq 100 fell as much as 4.7%, briefly 20% below its October record, before closing just 0.64% lower at 26,046.90; the S&P 500 closed 0.25% lower at 7,182.78 after being down 3.2% (CappNotes; Bloomberg). The data gave the Fed no way out: core PCE rose 3.3% year on year against a 3.2% forecast and Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3% annualised (CappNotes), so a December hike is priced at 97.8% (CME FedWatch). Tariffs are back: the US proposed Section 301 levies on 14 trading partners — up to 20% on China, which a senior official says could double to 40% if talks fail, and 15% on the EU, Japan and Korea (Bloomberg; CappNotes). Overnight two things moved the map. Nvidia has told its server partners to stop shipping to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia (FT), and lenders have frozen billions in data-centre loans tied to Chinese tenants (Bloomberg). And the Houthis say they struck Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia’s largest refinery; Brent settled up 3.51% at $94.61 (CappNotes). This morning the Bank of Korea hiked to 3.25% on a 5–2 vote with dots up to 3.75% (Seoul Economic Daily), and Asia is bouncing: Nikkei +1.56% and Hang Seng +2.05% at 05:30 GMT (FT). In South Africa the rand weakened to R17.70 (ECB) and the ten-year rose to 8.88% (TE) a day before Friday’s rating reviews. US markets are shut today.

Asia A rout, a hike and a bounce

United Kingdom Defensives up, tech and property down

The FTSE 100 fell 0.38% to 10,426.94 and the FTSE 250 0.93% to 23,637.00 (Share Talk). Imperial Brands rose 6.1% while Computacenter fell 8.2% and Auto Trader 3.3% on Amazon’s UK car platform. Henry Boot fell 14% after pushing land sales into 2027. The ten-year gilt eased to about 5.14% (FT; TE). The Bank of England publishes its Financial Stability Report today.

South Africa Miners and rand weaken into ratings day

The All Share fell 1.38% to 104,748.25 and the Top 40 1.55%; Resources fell 4.04%, while Prosus and Naspers hit new lows after results (Sharenet). The ECB fixed the rand at R17.70, its weakest this month, and the ten-year rose to 8.88% (TE). Ramaphosa won his Phala Phala court case, and the IMF warned that political gridlock could hurt confidence (Business Day).

United States Bear market touched, then a holiday

The S&P 500 fell 0.25% and the Nasdaq 100 0.64% after intraday drops of 3.2% and 4.7%; the Dow rose 0.33% and 370 S&P stocks rose (CappNotes). Core PCE rose 3.3%, GDP was revised to 4.3% and jobless claims fell to 189,000 (WSJ). S&P Global put Nvidia’s AA rating on watch negative. Markets are closed today and close early on Friday.
