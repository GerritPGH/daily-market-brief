Section 1What matters today

The bond rout has changed address. US Treasuries rallied on Thursday: the ten-year touched 5.34%, its highest since 2002, and closed at 5.24%, and the two-year fell 10bp to 4.79% (FT; Bloomberg, 1/10). The selling moved to Europe. France’s ten-year yield rose to 4.90% while Germany’s fell, which leaves the gap at about 137bp (Trading Economics, 1/10). Bloomberg’s John Authers puts that close to the record set in the 2011 euro crisis (opinion). The UK thirty-year gilt passed 6% for the first time since 1998 before closing at 5.92%, and the FTSE 100 fell 1.68% to its lowest since June. The dollar reached an 18-month high (WSJ), and the rand took the hit: 16.69 to the dollar, 1.5% weaker in a day, with JSE banks down 2.4%. Brent is back above $100, at $102.69, as the US sends more warships and troops to the Gulf. Hong Kong reopened 2.7% lower. US payrolls are due at 13:30 BST, and the consensus is +98,000 (Newsquawk).
