The tape paid up for perfection after all: Nvidia added $442bn in a single session — the second-largest one-day gain in market history — on record quarterly revenue of $96.2bn and ~70% growth guided for FY2028. But the same week, Alphabet has quietly erased more than $700bn from its May peak. Two names, one theme, opposite directions: the AI trade has become a dispersion trade, and the FT's number of the morning explains how it is being paid for — $72bn of zero-coupon convertible bonds issued this year, against a $73bn full-year record, meaning the buildout's cost of capital is now equity volatility rather than credit. Everything else waits for Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote as Fed chair, where the bond market is asking, unusually, to be spoken to more hawkishly: gilts at 5.067% are the developed world's high-yielder and Chancellor Healey has just been handed a ~£4bn hole before October's Budget. The best news of the day was South African and barely reported: producer inflation collapsed from 7.5% to 5.7% in one month. Watch Warsh; then watch SA CPI in September.
