Section 1What matters today

The weekend’s news should have lifted oil, and it did not. Houthi missiles hit Riyadh’s King Khalid airport on Saturday, the third attack on it in a week, killing 12 and injuring more than 300; Riyadh was hit again on Sunday and BP and Shell pulled out of this week’s energy conference there (FT FirstFT Asia, 11/10; Bloomberg Morning Briefing Asia, 11/10). Brent is nonetheless down 1.31% at $103.35 (FT panel, 05:32 BST). The reason is diesel. On Thursday President Trump said Russia would ship 4.8 million tonnes of it, about 36 million barrels, and the US Treasury issued a temporary licence within minutes (Yahoo Finance, 9/10). Heating-oil futures fell 4.1% on Friday (Trading Economics), and the US is now pressing Kyiv to stop striking Russian refineries, threatening its weapons-buying channel (FT FirstFT, 12/10). That is today’s first chain. Wall Street rose on Friday: the S&P 500 gained 0.59% to 7,811.51 and the VIX fell to 14.84 (TheStreet; Kiplinger; Cboe), but Verizon lost 8.75%, its worst day since 2002, after SpaceX bought nationwide mobile spectrum (stockanalysis.com; CNBC). The ten-year Treasury closed at 5.244% (Kiplinger), and Bloomberg says the term premium is the highest in more than a decade. This morning Tokyo is shut for a holiday and Seoul, back from its own, is down 3.08% at 6,422 (Trading Economics, intraday), on the same day customs data showed record chip exports of $12.1bn in the first ten days of October, up 171% on a year ago (Korea Times). In London the FTSE 100 rose 1.06% to 10,552.05 on Friday (Share Talk); the ten-year gilt is 5.413% and the IFS warns of a £35bn hole before the Budget (FT, 12/10). The JSE rose 1.32% to 108,524.77, the rand is 16.50 and the SA ten-year has fallen to 8.955% (Sharenet; Trading Economics). The EU has agreed to cap Chinese hybrid car imports, which is the second chain. US bank results start tomorrow and September CPI is on Wednesday. Gold is $4,188 and bitcoin $83,602 (Trading Economics, 12/10).
