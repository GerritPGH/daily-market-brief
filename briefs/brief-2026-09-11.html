Section 1What matters today

Three central banks were handed the same piece of evidence this week and drew three different conclusions, and the bond market decided all three of them were wrong. The evidence is oil: Brent settled 6.3% higher at $107.63 on Thursday and touched $109, the highest settlement in almost four months, after Houthi fighters seized the Yemeni port of Mokha and Saudi Arabia told OPEC its production last month was the lowest since 1990. The ECB hiked to a 2.50% deposit rate and Christine Lagarde called it “a no brainer”, decided unanimously. The Bank of Japan, per one of its own board members this week, must keep going so as not to fall behind. The Federal Reserve has not moved — and the market now puts a 25bp hike on 16 September at 62%, against 44% a month ago. What connects them is the price of money at the long end, and there the news is worse than any of it. The US ten-year is at 4.965%, its highest in nearly three years and three and a half basis points from five per cent. The thirty-year touched 5.37%, the highest since 2007. The ten-year gilt is at 5.407%. And the Japanese ten-year is at 2.988%, one and a bit basis points from 3%, a level it has not seen since 1996. Scott Bessent’s answer to this — a buyback he upsized to $6bn to break what he called a “fever” — failed to fill: Treasury accepted only $5.2bn of offers, and yields rose after the result. That is the single most important fact on this page, because a failed intervention is worse than no intervention. Meanwhile the thing that actually broke yesterday was not bonds but metals: platinum fell 5.6%, palladium 5.1% and silver 5.5% on the same session oil rose six, as the dollar surged on hike bets. That combination — import bill up, export book down — is why the rand is the worst-placed currency on this page at 16.2005. US CPI lands at 13:30 BST today, consensus +0.4% m/m and 3.4% y/y. It is the last inflation print the Fed sees before Wednesday.

Section 2World at a glance

Asia red

Tokyo staged a 2.7% intraday reversal and it is the tell of the week. The FT’s Asia panel had the Nikkei up 0.20% at 65,271 at 06:15 Singapore; by 05:30 in London it was −2.49% at 63,647, with Kioxia −6.9%, Advantest −6.2% and SoftBank −5.9%. That is an AI complex being sold for a rates reason, not a chip reason. Underneath, the JGB ten-year is at 2.988% — the biggest one-day move of any major curve, and a hair from 3% for the first time since 1996 — with a BoJ board member saying the Bank must raise further to avoid falling behind, and Scott Bessent publicly pressing Tokyo to tighten. The yen has given the whole thing back anyway: 154.38, against 153.37 on Thursday morning.

UK red

Britain now has the highest benchmark yield of the four majors on this page and it is not close: the ten-year gilt at 5.407% is 44bp above the US and 190bp above the Bund, and the thirty-year is at 5.95%, up 24bp on the month. The transmission is already visible where it hurts: the two-year fixed mortgage rate has jumped to 5.67% from 4.83%. The FTSE 100 closed at a seven-week low, and the mid-cap index — for which this brief finally found a free source this morning — is at 23,922.93, a fourteen-month low and well below the 24,506 it had been carrying. Markets now price roughly 90bp of further Bank of England tightening into a 17 September meeting where the Bank has not yet hiked once.

South Africa red

The worst possible session for a country that imports refined fuel and exports platinum group metals: it got hit at both ends within the same six hours. Brent +6.3%, platinum −6.39% on Sharenet’s spot, palladium −6.12%, silver −4.61%. Resource 20 −2.34%, Metals & Mining −2.21%, Anglo −5.71%, Implats −3.32%, Sibanye −3.12%. The rand fell 1.19% to 16.2005 and sterling reached R21.91. All of it lands on a current account that the Reserve Bank confirmed on Thursday has swung to a R205.5bn deficit (2.6% of GDP) from a R181.6bn surplus — the widest since 2019. One bright spot, and a big one: Transnet posted its first profit in four years, R4.6bn.

USA red

A fourth consecutive fall in the S&P 500 — the longest losing streak since June — and the reason is a failed auction rather than an earnings miss. Treasury’s upsized $6bn buyback took only $5.2bn of offers; the ten-year closed 11.7bp higher at 4.955% after the result, and the thirty-year hit 5.37%, its highest since 2007. PPI accelerated to +0.4% m/m from +0.1%. Donald Trump promised a $5,000 dividend to every US adult if Republicans hold Congress — a pledge costed at over $1tn, delivered into a market that has just told the Treasury Secretary it does not believe him. Pump prices are around $4.28 a gallon. The VIX rose 8.4% to 17.84 and is still, remarkably, below 18.
