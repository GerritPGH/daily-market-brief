Section 1What matters today

The AI trade broke on Thursday. Investors had cheered the Democratic sweep for a day; then they priced what it means for data centres. The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.36% to 26,629.81 and the S&P 500 4.36% to 7,353.73, its worst day since April 2025 (CappNotes; Bloomberg). Nvidia lost more than $400bn of value, the VIX jumped to 28.18 and the two-year Treasury fell 12bp to 4.46% (FT; Cboe; Treasury). This morning the selling reached Asia: KOSPI tripped an 8% circuit breaker and the Nikkei is down about 6% (TE; FT). The money did not leave; it moved. The JSE rose 3.67% on the AI unwind and an election in which the ANC is losing every big metro, and foreign investors bought $1.6bn of Indian shares, their biggest day since 2021 (Sharenet; Business Day; Business Standard). The Bank of England raised rates to 4% by 8–1; Catherine Mann wanted 50bp (BoE). That vote kills our oil-to-BoE chain. Two new chains follow the money: into India, and into sterling as the Fed’s next hike looks less certain. US payrolls are out at 13:30 GMT.

United Kingdom A hawkish hike, a calm FTSE

The BoE raised Bank Rate to 4% by 8–1, with Mann voting for 4.25%, and expects CPI near 5% by mid-2027 (BoE). The FTSE 100 gave up a 166-point gain to close −0.43% at 10,782.66 (Share Talk). The ten-year gilt was flat at 5.14% (TE). The BoE is also asking lenders about risky data-centre loans (Bloomberg).

FTSE 100 10,783 −0.43% · 10y 5.14% flat · GBP/USD 1.3459 −0.30%

South Africa Election relief and the AI unwind

The JSE reopened with its best day in months: All Share +3.67%, banks +5.31%, Capitec at a record (Sharenet). Early results show the ANC losing its majority in every big metro, with an FT-reported projection of 32% against 46% in 2021 (Business Day; FT). The DA has won Cape Town and leads Johannesburg (Daily Maverick).
